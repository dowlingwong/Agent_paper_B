\caption{\textbf{A failure score is not an allocation rule.}
Given capacity to verify only a fraction $q$ of checkpoints, the standard
protocol audits the top $q$ by predicted failure risk.
\textbf{(a)} Risk order and verification-advantage order are different
permutations of the same population: at budget $b=3$ the risk rule selects
$\{A,B,C\}$ and captures advantage $4$, while the advantage rule selects
$\{C,G,E\}$ and captures $8$.
\textbf{(b)} The gap between the two capture curves is the budget-matched
risk-ranking regret. It vanishes at \emph{every} capacity if and only if
baseline failure risk is ordinally sufficient for verification advantage
(\cref{thm:ordinal}) --- a sharp null this design is built to test.
\emph{Schematic: no experimental data are shown.}}
\label{fig:teaser}
