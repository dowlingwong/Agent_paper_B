\paragraph{False and missed keeps.}
Pooled over nodes, \COneFalseKeepSOne{} of the keeps in sweep~1 and \COneFalseKeepSTwo{} in sweep~2 had an audited increment that was not positive. Across nodes the rate ranges over \COneFalseKeepRange. Figure~\ref{fig:c1} plots it against each node's noise level. \TBD{One sentence on the relation in the figure, no stronger than eight nodes allow.} Our false-keep rate is a share of keeps, whereas \citet{jalaian2026noise} report the share of truly neutral candidates that a rule keeps, so the two are not directly comparable. The missed-keep rate is \COneMissedKeepSOne{} in sweep~1 and \COneMissedKeepSTwo{} in sweep~2.

\paragraph{Record bias.}
The record bias, the recorded gain of the incumbent minus its audited gain, is \COneRecordBiasSOne{} in sweep~1 and \COneRecordBiasSTwo{} in sweep~2, in units of $\sigma_{\mathrm{node}}$. Proposition~\ref{prop:bias} states that a keep leaves the record with a positive expected error whenever the candidate's error has conditional mean zero and a discard was possible. The measurement gives the size of the bias in the logged campaigns. It is not a test of the proposition, which is a mathematical statement. With a noise component shared by the campaign, the zero-mean condition can fail (Section~\ref{sec:theory-bias}). \TBD{Sign and size per node; name every node on which the bias is not positive.}

\paragraph{Wasted runs.}
Runs on duplicates, no-ops and invalid proposals take \COneWastedSOne{} of the runs in sweep~1 and \COneWastedSTwo{} in sweep~2. Duplicates alone take \COneDupSOne{} and \COneDupSTwo. \TBD{Duplicate share with and without memory, per sweep; per node and memory mode in an appendix table.} \citet{chen2026visible} report duplicate shares in a deterministic setting (Section~\ref{sec:related}). Their loop, proposer and denominator differ from ours, so we give the numbers side by side and do not compare them formally. On a node with bit-exact runs, a duplicate returns the metric already in the ledger and cannot be kept under the default rule (Proposition~\ref{prop:app-rules}), so its run is lost whatever the noise. The rule layer of A2 skips such proposals; Sections~\ref{sec:results-c3} and~\ref{sec:results-closed} include its value offline and in the closed loop. On the exam data, the rule layer had less to skip for the main proposer than for the sweep-1 manager (Appendix~\ref{app:exam}). \TBD{Whether the two sweeps show the same ordering, descriptively.}

\paragraph{Controls.}
Two controls per node support the reading of Table~\ref{tab:c1} (\TBD{appendix table of the controls}). The positive control asks whether the audit can see a known effect. For each node, it takes the single edits of the default configuration whose effects were measured with 30 seeds in a separate noise-floor run, and gives the probability that an audit with $K=10$ seeds, read with the audit statistic that C1 uses, detects each edit. The probability is estimated by resampling 10 of the 30 seeds. It is \COnePositiveControl. The negative control asks whether a rule keeps an edit that changes nothing: for each node, and for the default loop, A1 and A2, it gives the share of no-op proposals kept. On a node with bit-exact runs a strict keep rule keeps no no-op by construction, so only nodes whose runs are not bit-exact carry information: \COneNegativeControl. \TBD{If a node has no no-op proposal in a sweep, its cells are empty and the table says so.} Neither control is a test, and neither reads a test label or an evaluation value. \TBD{Keep this paragraph only if the controls are finished and reviewed by 3 Jan 2027; the positive control comes first.}

\paragraph{The dispersion bound, as an estimate.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:app-gap} bounds the loss of a keep decision made with a constant threshold in place of the per-proposal threshold. The loss is quadratic in the distance between the two thresholds. A per-proposal forecast can therefore add much to the keep decision only where the per-proposal thresholds spread widely around the node's constant threshold. We evaluate the upper bound with the fitted forecasts of A3 and A5 in place of the true parameters, against A2's constant threshold, on \TBD{proposal population}. Scaled to 20 proposals and in units of $\sigma_{\mathrm{node}}$, it is \DispBoundAThree{} with A3's forecast and \DispBoundAFive{} with A5's (\TBD{per node in an appendix table}). We read this number as an estimate, not as a bound on what a better forecaster could add. The proposition bounds the loss in terms of the true parameters; with fitted parameters, the error of the estimate is not controlled. A forecast that is too smooth understates the spread of the per-proposal thresholds, and so understates the room for improvement. The bound covers the keep threshold only, at fixed execution decisions; screening, whose one-step gain is $(g-V^*_n)^+$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:onestep}), lies outside it. Its campaign-level reading needs (NF) and (NI), which the loops we run do not satisfy, so we read it per proposal. The estimate uses forecasts and the measured noise only and reads no label. It is descriptive and is not part of the preregistration. \TBD{Check status of Proposition~\ref{prop:app-gap} and of the value formula it rests on (Proposition~\ref{prop:app-threshold}): author-checked, AI-checked under the proof policy, or not checked.}
