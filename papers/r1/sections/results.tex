Under the Holm step-down, the pass rule is met by \GatePassingArms. The claim that the preregistration supports is limited to a passing arm: on the nodes of the read and at the declared run price, the arm's forecast, fed to the one-step rule, earns more offline one-step utility than A2's constant forecast. The point IQM of a passing arm is \TBD{above or below} the practical size of 0.1 declared with the gate. \subsection{Control errors of the default loop}
\label{sec:results-c1}

The logged sweeps show, as supporting evidence, what the default loop gets wrong. Pooled over nodes, \COneFalseKeepSOne{} of the keeps in sweep~1 and \COneFalseKeepSTwo{} in sweep~2 had an audited increment that was not positive. The recorded gain of the incumbent exceeded its audited gain by \COneRecordBiasSOne{} and \COneRecordBiasSTwo{} $\sigma_{\mathrm{node}}$. Runs on duplicates, no-ops and invalid proposals took \COneWastedSOne{} and \COneWastedSTwo{} of the runs. Details are in Appendix~\ref{app:c1}.
