\paragraph{Summary (alternative A).}
The preregistered gate passes: \GateVerdict. The IQM of gap closed over the eligible nodes is \GateIQM, and the probability of improvement is \GatePoI, for the arm named in Table~\ref{tab:ladder}. The gate was read once, on \TOneNodesUsed{} and the seven primary T0 nodes, with \GateEligibleNodes. A learned per-proposal forecast therefore adds offline one-step utility beyond the rule-based index policy A2 on these nodes. Three limits apply to this statement. The utility is a proxy for closed-loop value that relies on decision independence (Section~\ref{sec:problem}). The gate had low power, so the size of the effect is uncertain, and we report its interval. The closed-loop check (Table~\ref{tab:closed}) is small and covers the rule-based and tabular arms only. C1 (Table~\ref{tab:c1}) and C2 (Section~\ref{sec:theory}) do not depend on the gate.

\paragraph{Summary (alternative B).}
The preregistered gate does not pass: \GateVerdict. The IQM of gap closed is \GateIQM, and the probability of improvement is \GatePoI. The gate had low power: in its calibration simulation the mean two-arm power was \GatePowerMain{} at an effect size of \GatePowerEffect{} (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). The result is therefore not evidence that learning adds nothing; it is the absence of a demonstrated gain. We report the effect sizes of every arm with their intervals (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}), and we do not interpret differences between learned arms. Three parts of the paper remain supported and do not depend on the gate:
\begin{itemize}
\item C1, the measured control errors of the default loop (Table~\ref{tab:c1});
\item C2, the theory (Section~\ref{sec:theory}), subject to the proof check;
\item the ladder up to the rule-based arms: the gains of the constant-prior index policy A1 and of the rule layer A2 over the default loop A0, which are reported descriptively with intervals in Table~\ref{tab:ladder} and in the closed-loop check (Table~\ref{tab:closed}).
\end{itemize}
