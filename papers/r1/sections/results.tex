\paragraph{Summary.}
\ifGatePassed
The preregistered gate passes for \GatePassingArms. It was read once, after the arms were frozen, on the seven primary T0 nodes and \TOneNodesUsed; \GateEligibleNodes{} entered the IQM. For a passing arm, the lower bound of the IQM of gap closed is above 0 and the lower bound of the PoI is above 0.5 at the arm's Holm level (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). On these nodes, the arm's per-proposal forecast, fed to the closed-form rule, therefore earns more offline one-step utility than the constant forecast of the index policy A2. Three limits apply. The utility is a proxy for closed-loop value that relies on decision independence, and the loops we run do not satisfy it (Section~\ref{sec:problem}). The gate has low power, and a test that passes at low power tends to overstate the effect, so we read the lower bounds rather than the point values. The closed-loop comparison of the learned arms (Section~\ref{sec:results-closed}) is on T0 only, with one repetition. C1 and C2 do not depend on the gate.
\else

The offline data do not show that a learned per-proposal forecast adds one-step utility beyond the index policy A2; they do not show that it adds nothing. \paragraph{False and missed keeps.}
Pooled over nodes, \COneFalseKeepSOne{} of the keeps in sweep~1 and \COneFalseKeepSTwo{} in sweep~2 had an audited increment that was not positive. Across nodes the rate ranges over \COneFalseKeepRange. Figure~\ref{fig:c1} plots it against each node's noise level. \TBD{One sentence on the relation in the figure, no stronger than eight nodes allow.} Our false-keep rate is a share of keeps, whereas \citet{jalaian2026noise} report the share of truly neutral candidates that a rule keeps, so the two are not directly comparable. The missed-keep rate is \COneMissedKeepSOne{} in sweep~1 and \COneMissedKeepSTwo{} in sweep~2.

\paragraph{Record bias.}
The record bias, the recorded gain of the incumbent minus its audited gain, is \COneRecordBiasSOne{} in sweep~1 and \COneRecordBiasSTwo{} in sweep~2, in units of $\sigma_{\mathrm{node}}$. Proposition~\ref{prop:bias} states that a keep leaves the record with a positive expected error whenever the candidate's error has conditional mean zero and a discard was possible. The measurement shows how large the effect is in the logged campaigns. It is not a test of the proposition, which is a mathematical statement. With a noise component shared by the campaign, the zero-mean condition can fail (Section~\ref{sec:theory-bias}). \TBD{Sign and size per node; name every node on which the bias is not positive.}

\paragraph{Wasted runs.}
Runs on duplicates, no-ops and invalid proposals take \COneWastedSOne{} of the runs in sweep~1 and \COneWastedSTwo{} in sweep~2. Duplicates alone take \COneDupSOne{} and \COneDupSTwo. \TBD{Duplicate share with and without memory, per sweep; per node and memory mode in an appendix table.} \citet{chen2026visible} report duplicate shares in a deterministic setting (Section~\ref{sec:related}). Their loop, proposer and denominator differ from ours, so we give the numbers side by side and do not compare them formally. On a node with bit-exact runs, a duplicate returns the metric already in the ledger and cannot be kept under the default rule (Proposition~\ref{prop:app-rules}), so its run is lost whatever the noise. The rule layer of A2 skips such proposals; Sections~\ref{sec:results-c3} and~\ref{sec:results-closed} include its value offline and in the closed loop. On the exam data, the rule layer had less to skip for the main proposer than for the sweep-1 manager (Appendix~\ref{app:exam}). \TBD{Whether the two sweeps show the same ordering, descriptively.}

\paragraph{Controls.}
Two controls per node support the reading of Table~\ref{tab:c1} (\TBD{appendix table of the controls}). The positive control asks whether the audit can see a known effect. For each node, it takes the single edits of the default configuration whose effects were measured with 30 seeds in the accepted noise-floor runs, and gives the probability that an audit with $K=10$ seeds, read with the audit statistic that C1 uses, detects each edit. The probability is estimated by resampling 10 of the 30 seeds. It is \COnePositiveControl. The negative control asks whether a rule keeps an edit that changes nothing: for each node, and for the default loop, A1 and A2, it gives the share of no-op proposals kept. On a node with bit-exact runs a strict keep rule keeps no no-op by construction, so only nodes whose runs are not bit-exact carry information: \COneNegativeControl. \TBD{If a node has no no-op proposal in a sweep, its cells are empty and the table says so.} Neither control is a test, and neither reads a test label or an evaluation value. \TBD{Keep this paragraph only if the controls are finished and reviewed by 3 Jan 2027; the positive control comes first.}
