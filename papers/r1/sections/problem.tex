\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\paragraph{The governed loop.}
A campaign runs on one \emph{node}: a training task with an editable configuration or script, and a validation metric. At trial $n=1,\dots,N$, a language-model \emph{manager} (the proposer) proposes a bounded edit $x_n$ to the incumbent. A control plane, which is separate from the manager, owns two decisions:
\begin{itemize}
\item at epoch E1, before a run is spent, it executes the edit ($a_n=1$) or skips it ($a_n=0$);
\item at epoch E2, after an execution, it keeps the candidate as the new incumbent ($k_n=1$) or discards it ($k_n=0$), with $k_n\le a_n$.
\end{itemize}
An execution consumes one worker run from a budget of $W$ runs, and $N$ caps the number of manager calls. Every trial, whether kept, discarded, skipped or failed, is appended to a ledger. The \emph{default loop} executes every proposal once and keeps a candidate when its observed metric beats the incumbent's recorded value.

\paragraph{Observation model.}
Let $\Delta_n$ be the true increment of the candidate over the incumbent, and $I_n$ the event that the edit leaves the incumbent's effective configuration unchanged (a \emph{no-op}, $\Delta_n=0$). An execution reveals $H_n=\Delta_n+\eta_n$, where $\eta_n$ is comparison noise with per-comparison scale $\sigma_n$. Increments are standardised by a node-level noise scale $\sigma_{\mathrm{node}}$. A skip reveals nothing. An edit that repeats an earlier evaluated configuration other than the incumbent is a \emph{duplicate}. The information before execution is
\[
\mathcal{O}_n=\sigma\bigl(x_{1:n},\,(a_j,k_j,a_jH_j)_{j<n},\,U\bigr),
\]
where $U$ collects all control-plane randomness.

\paragraph{The campaign outcome.}
The \emph{audited increment} of a configuration over another is measured by retraining both on fresh seeds that played no part in generating, filtering or selecting either (Section~\ref{sec:setup-data}). The outcome $Y$ of a campaign is the audited increment of its final incumbent over its start configuration, divided by $\sigma_{\mathrm{node}}$, at a fixed budget of $W$ worker runs and a fixed cap of $N$ manager calls. This is the quantity the headline question is about. The improvement that a campaign records for itself is not $Y$: it is selected on noise (Proposition~\ref{prop:bias}).

\paragraph{A priced objective for analysis.}
For analysis we relax the hard budget to the objective
\[
J_g(\pi)=\mathbb{E}^\pi\Bigl[\textstyle\sum_{n\le N}(\Delta_n k_n-g\,a_n)\Bigr],
\]
which prices a run at a declared \emph{run price} $g$. It is the Lagrangian relaxation of $\mathbb{E}[\sum_n a_n]\le W$. The run price is declared in advance and is not tuned.

\paragraph{Three levels of evidence.}
We keep three kinds of result apart.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Development.} Calibration and forecast skill on training and development instances. They select and calibrate the arms and support no claim.
\item \emph{Offline one-step utility.} For an arm and a logged campaign, the weighted mean over the campaign's logged proposals of the realised one-step value $\Delta^{\mathrm{aud}}_n k_n-g\,a_n$ under the arm's decisions, scaled to 20 proposals. This is the quantity of the preregistered gate. A skip earns $0$ instead of $-g$, and the freed run is not re-spent.
\item \emph{Closed loop.} The outcome $Y$ when the arm controls a fresh campaign.
\end{itemize}
The offline utility is a proxy for the closed-loop outcome only under the two conditions of Section~\ref{sec:theory}. In a real campaign a skipped run is spent on the next proposal, and a decision changes what the manager sees next (Appendix~\ref{app:design}). We therefore measure the closed loop directly and report the agreement between the two levels as a finding.
