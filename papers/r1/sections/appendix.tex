\section{Further Theory Statements}
\label{app:theory}

\TBD{Author proof check pending for every statement in this appendix. The statements follow an independent audit of 27 Oct 2026 and carry the assumptions that the audit added. Two claims of the earlier draft that the audit found wrong are not made here; the remarks say which.}

Throughout, $\Phi$ and $\phi$ are the standard normal distribution function and density, $\psi(z)=\phi(z)-z\Phi(-z)$, and $\lambda(a)=\phi(a)/\Phi(-a)$. Conditional identities hold almost surely.

\subsection{One decision with a fixed prior}

Let $\Delta\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\tau^2)$ and $H=\Delta+\eta$ with $\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_d^2)$ independent of $\Delta$. Fresh replicate errors are independent of each other and of $(\Delta,\eta)$.

\begin{proposition}[Threshold value]\label{prop:app-threshold}
For $\tau>0$ and $\sigma_d>0$, the rule that keeps when $H>t$ has value $V(t;\mu,\tau,\sigma_d)$. Its unique maximiser is $t^*=-\mu\sigma_d^2/\tau^2$. The threshold $t=0$ is optimal if and only if $\mu=0$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Edge cases.} If $\sigma_d=0$ and $\tau>0$, then $t=0$ is the unique finite optimal threshold for every $\mu$. If $\tau=0$ the formula for $t^*$ is undefined: a known $\mu>0$ means always keep, a known $\mu<0$ means always discard, and at $\mu=0$ all rules tie.

\begin{proposition}[Value of verification]\label{prop:app-voi}
Let $\bar D$ be the mean of $r\ge1$ paired fresh comparisons, jointly Gaussian with $(\Delta,H)$. Let $m=\mathbb{E}[\Delta\mid H]$ and $q^2=\mathrm{Var}(\mathbb{E}[\Delta\mid H,\bar D]\mid H)$. The value of observing $\bar D$ before a Bayes keep decision is $q\,\psi(|m|/q)$ for $q>0$, and $0$ for $q=0$. It decreases in $|m|$ and increases in $q$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Remark.} We make no claim of diminishing returns in $r$. The posterior quantity $q_r^2$ is concave in $r$, but the value is convex in $q$ for $m\neq0$, and the increments of the value can increase with $r$. The value is that of a Bayes response to the verification result. A fixed response rule, such as keeping when $\bar D>0$, can be worth less.

\begin{proposition}[Which noise verification removes]\label{prop:app-split}
Let the comparison noise have a training-seed part with variance $\sigma_t^2$ and a split-seed part with variance $\sigma_s^2$, and let $\tau>0$ and $\sigma_s>0$. If verification re-draws only the training seed, its value tends to zero as $\sigma_t\to0$. If it re-draws both seeds, its value has a positive limit for each fixed $H$. The limit is pointwise in $H$, not uniform.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Acceptance against a biased record]\label{prop:app-record}
Let the incumbent's recorded error $e$ and a candidate's error be centred bivariate normal with common variance $\sigma^2>0$ and correlation $\varrho\in(-1,1)$, given the history. For a proposal with fixed true increment $\delta$,
\[
\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{keep}\mid e)=\Phi\!\Bigl(\frac{\delta-(1-\varrho)e}{\sigma\sqrt{1-\varrho^2}}\Bigr),
\]
which is strictly decreasing in $e$. Suppose further that every trial of a stretch is a null proposal with an independent fresh $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ error, and that the initial record is finite. Then the record's error tends to infinity almost surely, and the conditional keep probability of a later proposal with fixed $\delta$ tends to zero.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Remark.} The second part describes a hypothetical null stretch. It is not a statement about an arbitrary campaign.

\begin{proposition}[Verification under a run price]\label{prop:app-verify}
Let $v=\mathrm{Var}(\Delta\mid H)$. The value of verification with $r$ paired runs is at most $q\phi(0)\le\sqrt{v}\,\phi(0)$. If a run has an externally fixed additive price $g$, verifying is worth its cost $2rg$ only if the value exceeds $2rg$, and it never is when $2rg\ge\sqrt{v}\,\phi(0)$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Remark.} This is a one-step comparison under a fixed price and a Bayes response. It is not a hard-budget optimum, and it does not show that a fixed budget is better spent on fresh proposals.

\subsection{Per-proposal statements}

\begin{proposition}[Value of a per-proposal threshold]\label{prop:app-gap}
In the closed form of Proposition~\ref{prop:onestep}, with $\tau_n>0$ and $\pi_n<1$, let $\tilde t$ be any finite threshold that is measurable with respect to the pre-execution information, and let $f_n$ be the $\mathcal{N}(\mu_n,s_n^2)$ density. Then
\begin{align*}
&V^*_n-(1-\pi_n)V(\tilde t;\mu_n,\tau_n,\sigma_n)\\
&\quad=(1-\pi_n)\frac{\tau_n^2}{s_n^2}\Bigl|\int_{\tilde t}^{t^*_n}f_n(u)\,(t^*_n-u)\,du\Bigr|\\
&\quad\le(1-\pi_n)\frac{\tau_n^2\,(t^*_n-\tilde t)^2}{2\sqrt{2\pi}\,s_n^3}.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

The loss from a wrong threshold is quadratic in the threshold error. A constant threshold per node therefore loses little unless the per-proposal thresholds are dispersed. An estimate of this bound from a pilot forecaster bounds what a better forecaster could add only if the pilot's own errors are controlled; the algebra alone does not give that.

\begin{proposition}[Rule-detectable waste]\label{prop:app-rules}
Consider a node whose metric is bit-exact at fixed seeds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If a proposal is a detectable no-op, then $\pi_n=1$ and $V^*_n=0$, and skipping is optimal for every $g>0$.
\item[(ii)] If a proposal repeats an earlier evaluated configuration other than the incumbent, a run returns the metric already in the ledger. It reveals nothing, and its value is $-g<0$ for every $g>0$.
\item[(iii)] Under the default keep rule the recorded incumbent value never decreases, so such a duplicate can never be kept.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

These cases need no learning. They form the rule layer of Section~\ref{sec:method}. A duplicate's true increment is generally not zero, so it does not receive the no-op label.

\paragraph{Edge cases of Proposition~\ref{prop:onestep}.}
If $\pi_n=1$ then $V^*_n=0$; skipping is optimal for $g>0$ and ties with executing at $g=0$. If $\tau_n=0$ for a non-no-op, the increment $\mu_n$ is known, the rule keeps when $m_n>0$, and $V^*_n=(1-\pi_n)\mu_n^+$. With $0<\pi_n<1$, the observation separates no-ops from other proposals if and only if the comparison value of a non-no-op has no probability mass at zero; $\mu_n\neq0$ alone is not enough. Without separation and with $\mu_n>0$, the rule keeps every proposal, no-ops included, and the value is unchanged because keeping a no-op gains nothing.

\paragraph{The shared-error model.}
Under the model of Proposition~\ref{prop:bias}, the comparison noise is $\eta_n=(\varrho_n-1)e+\sigma_{c,n}\xi_n$. Given $\mathcal{O}_n$ it has mean $(\varrho_n-1)\mathbb{E}[e\mid\mathcal{O}_n]$, which is generally not zero. The closed form of Proposition~\ref{prop:onestep} then applies to the comparison corrected by $(1-\varrho_n)\mathbb{E}[e\mid\mathcal{O}_n]$, with $(1-\varrho_n)^2\mathrm{Var}(e\mid\mathcal{O}_n)$ added to the noise variance. It holds exactly only if the posterior of $e$ is Gaussian and $e$ is conditionally independent of $\Delta_n$. Verification is an information action in this model, of the kind that Proposition~\ref{prop:failures}(b) prices.

\paragraph{Summed gains need both conditions.}
The sum in Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}(3) is valid under (NF) and (NI) together. Under (NF) alone the screening gain can be negative. In the example of Proposition~\ref{prop:failures}(b) with $\alpha=1$, $\beta=10$, $\kappa=1$ and $g=0.5$, the one-step rule has value $2.3209$ and always executing with a Bayes keep has value $3.3884$, so the actual screening gain is $-1.0674$, while the formula evaluated with the one-step values gives $+0.1011$. Without both conditions, campaign gains must be measured.

\paragraph{Bias of offline evaluation.}
In a sweep every proposal is executed. A skipping controller that is evaluated offline therefore sees histories that contain results it would not have observed online. For a Bayes one-step controller under (NF), offline evaluation of skipping is optimistic, and the bias is zero under (NI). For a learned forecaster the sign is not determined, even under (NI). The actual loop lies outside (NF), so for it the sign is not determined either.

\subsection{The two failure examples}

\paragraph{Feedback (Proposition~\ref{prop:failures}(a)).}
Take $N=2$, $g=0$ and no observation noise. Trial 1 proposes a known increment $-\alpha<0$. If it is kept, trial 2 draws its increment from $\mathcal{N}(\beta,\tau^2)$; if it is discarded, from $\mathcal{N}(0,\tau^2)$. The one-step rule discards trial 1 and earns $\tau\phi(0)$. Keeping earns $-\alpha+\beta\Phi(\beta/\tau)+\tau\phi(\beta/\tau)$. With $\tau=1$ and $\beta=2$ the difference is $1.60955-\alpha$, and it grows without bound in $\beta$.

\paragraph{Information (Proposition~\ref{prop:failures}(b)).}
In the example of Section~\ref{sec:theory}, the one-step rule skips the probe when $g>\alpha\phi(0)$ and has value $(\beta\phi(0)/\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}-g)^+$. The probe-first policy has value
\[
\alpha\phi(0)-g+\beta\phi(g/\beta)-g\,\Phi(-g/\beta).
\]
The optimality gap of the one-step rule is the positive part of the difference. Table~\ref{tab:ex-info} gives closed-form values.

\subsection{Offline checks and synthetic controls}

Five offline checks of observable consequences of (NF) and (NI) are part of the analysis plan. None needs new labels.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Display audit.} Verify what the manager is shown in the no-memory mode. If it is shown anything that depends on past decisions, the non-reactive-proposer condition fails there too.
\item \emph{Duplicate re-proposals.} Count re-proposals of configurations that a skip would have left unevaluated. This bears on the policy-invariant trial sequence.
\item \emph{Masking test for (NI).} Compare a forecaster with and without executed results in its history, both given the same proposal history, out of sample. An improvement beyond estimation error is evidence against (NI). Its absence does not establish (NI).
\item \emph{Residuals within an incumbent spell.} Compare comparison residuals within and across incumbent spells, and after keeps against fresh incumbents. This probes the shared-error mechanism.
\item \emph{Interaction of edits.} Compare the audited increments of the same edit over different incumbents, on the pairs that the sweeps provide.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\TBD{Author proof check pending for the proofs as written here. The source proofs of Proposition~\ref{prop:onestep}, Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} and Proposition~\ref{prop:failures}(b) passed the author's check of 16 Nov 2026; the text below restates them and has not been checked.}

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:onestep}.}
The action $a_n$ is $\mathcal{O}_n$-measurable, and $k_n=a_nk_n$ is measurable with respect to $\mathcal{O}_n\vee\sigma(H_n)$. By the tower property,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_nk_n\mid\mathcal{O}_n]&=\mathbb{E}[k_nm_n\mid\mathcal{O}_n]\\
&\le a_n\,\mathbb{E}[m_n^+\mid\mathcal{O}_n]=a_nV^*_n ,
\end{align*}
with equality for $k_n=a_n\mathbf{1}\{m_n>0\}$. This is (i). For (ii) and (iii), compare $V^*_n-g$ with $0$ and use $\max(V^*_n-g,0)-(V^*_n-g)=(g-V^*_n)^+$. For the closed form, the assumptions make $\{H_n=0\}$ coincide almost surely with the no-op event, on which $\Delta_n=0$. On $\{H_n\neq0\}$, $(\Delta_n,H_n)$ is bivariate normal given $\mathcal{O}_n$, with means $(\mu_n,\mu_n)$, $\mathrm{Var}(H_n)=s_n^2$ and covariance $\tau_n^2$. Hence $m_n=\mu_n+(\tau_n^2/s_n^2)(H_n-\mu_n)$, which is positive if and only if $H_n>t^*_n$. The value on the non-no-op event is $V(t^*_n;\mu_n,\tau_n,\sigma_n)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:app-threshold}. The argument does not use any independence between $U$ and the trial outcomes. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:bias}.}
Given the history, $e$ and $\Delta_n$, the candidate error is $\varepsilon_n\sim\mathcal{N}(\varrho_ne,\sigma_{c,n}^2)$, and a keep is the event $\varepsilon_n>e-\Delta_n$. The mean of a normal variable with mean $m$ and standard deviation $\sigma_c$, truncated from below at $c$, is $m+\sigma_c\lambda((c-m)/\sigma_c)$. With $m=\varrho_ne$ and $c=e-\Delta_n$ this is the stated formula. The inequalities $\lambda(a)>0$ and $\lambda(a)>a$ for all real $a$ give $\mathbb{E}[e'\mid\mathrm{keep}]>m$ and $\mathbb{E}[e'\mid\mathrm{keep}]>m+\sigma_c\,(c-m)/\sigma_c=c$. The bound is pointwise in $(e,\Delta_n,\varrho_n)$, so it holds for every proposal law that preserves the noise kernel. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}.}
Write $Y_n=(\mathbf{1}\{I_n\},\Delta_n,\eta_n)$ and $\mathcal{G}_n=\sigma(x_{1:n},Y_{<n},U)$.

\emph{Step 1.} By induction, each $a_j$ and $k_j$ is a function of $(x_{1:j},Y_{\le j},U)$, and $H_j$ is a function of $Y_j$. Hence $\mathcal{O}_n\subseteq\mathcal{G}_n$. (NF) gives independence of $U$ and $\omega_{1:N}$, so by weak union $U$ is conditionally independent of $Y_n$ given $(x_{1:n},Y_{<n})$. (NI) gives conditional independence of $Y_n$ and $Y_{<n}$ given $x_{1:n}$. By contraction, $Y_n$ is conditionally independent of $\mathcal{G}_n$ given $x_{1:n}$. Since $\sigma(x_{1:n})\subseteq\mathcal{O}_n\subseteq\mathcal{G}_n$, the tower property gives $\mathbb{E}[f(Y_n)\mid\mathcal{O}_n]=\mathbb{E}[f(Y_n)\mid x_{1:n}]$ for every bounded $f$. Weak union then gives conditional independence of $\Delta_n$ and $\mathcal{O}_n$ given $(x_{1:n},H_n)$, which is part (1).

\emph{Step 2.} Proposition~\ref{prop:onestep}(i) and (ii) give, for every policy and every $n$,
\[
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_nk_n-g\,a_n\mid\mathcal{O}_n]\le a_n(V^*_n-g)\le(V^*_n-g)^+ .
\]

\emph{Step 3.} The one-step policy attains equality in both inequalities at every $n$. The bounds can be added across trials for two reasons. (NF) gives every policy the same law of the proposal sequence. By part (1), which uses (NI), the executed history does not change the current posterior once the proposal history is given. So each $V^*_n$ is the same function of $x_{1:n}$ under every policy, with the same law, and $\sum_n\mathbb{E}[(V^*_n-g)^+]$ bounds $J_g$ for every policy. This gives part (2). For part (3), always executing has finer information, but by part (1) its $V^*_n$ is the same function of $x_{1:n}$, and it earns $\sum_n\mathbb{E}[V^*_n-g]$ under the same law. \qed

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:failures}.}
The computations are in Appendix~\ref{app:theory}. For (b), with $N=2$ and (NF), the only choice that the one-step rule does not make is whether to run the probe, so the optimum is the larger of the two policy values. For fixed $\alpha$, $g$ and $\kappa>0$ the difference of the two values is $\beta\phi(0)(1-1/\sqrt{1+\kappa^2})+\alpha\phi(0)-g/2+o(1)$ as $\beta\to\infty$. \qed
