\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Nodes and noise floors.}
We use eight CPU training nodes:
\begin{itemize}
\item two multilayer-perceptron tasks (digits, and a synthetic tabular task);
\item six OpenML classification tasks \citep{vanschoren2013openml}: breast-w, ionosphere, wdbc, credit-approval, sonar and climate-model-simulation-crashes.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Noise level.} On each node, $\sigma_{\mathrm{node}}$ is the median joint-seed standard deviation of the paired difference over material edits, measured with 30 seeds per condition. Increments are reported in $z=\Delta/\sigma_{\mathrm{node}}$.

\emph{Qualification.} A node qualifies if the default keep rule's mean probability of a wrong decision over the node's material edits is at least 10\%, with a minimum number of material edits.

\emph{Repair and replacement.} Four candidate nodes failed a pre-collection liveness check: no proposal improved on their near-ceiling start configuration. They were repaired by a pre-declared start-configuration rule and re-measured. Three then qualified; the fourth was replaced by a pre-declared reserve node.

\paragraph{Manager and campaigns.}
The manager is a pinned open-weight model, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct \citep{hui2024qwencoder}, at 4-bit (Q4\_K\_M) quantization and served by llama.cpp \citep{llamacpp}. Its proposals follow a fixed structured schema. \TBD{Confirm structure-only JSON-schema decoding in the recorded manager path.}

Each node has 18 campaign seeds, and each seed is run under both memory modes: the manager either sees an append-only summary of earlier trials (primary) or has no memory (contrast). This gives 36 campaigns per node, each with a budget of $W=20$ worker runs. In the DAC terms of Section~\ref{sec:problem}, the node is the instance and its campaigns are repeated runs on it.

\paragraph{Labels and splits.}
The non-inert, non-duplicate proposals are audited on $K=10$ fresh joint seeds, disjoint from the campaign seeds. \TBD{All audited, or a random sample reweighted by inverse sampling probability.} The ten audit seeds have separate roles:
\begin{itemize}
\item the first five (7001--7005) form the training label, a 12-bin soft label over $z$;
\item the last five (7006--7010) give the evaluation value.
\end{itemize}
Every audit seed is a task-level joint-seed retraining of the candidate and the incumbent. The evaluation seeds are to be excluded from every decision-model training, tuning or selection step. \TBD{The loader guard that enforces this boundary is to be implemented and independently reviewed.}

The width of the soft label differs by model class:
\begin{itemize}
\item for the decision models it is the pooled node noise, $\sigma_{\mathrm{node}}/\sqrt5$;
\item for A1--A3 it is the audit standard error.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Configuration policies.}
The policies follow the static, per-instance and dynamic levels of Section~\ref{sec:problem}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Static:} the default loop (A0).
\item \emph{Per-instance:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item a constant prior fitted on the node's own campaigns (A1);
  \item a context-only prior predicted from node descriptors, fitted across the training nodes (A1-ctx).
  \end{itemize}
\item \emph{Dynamic:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item A1 plus a rule layer that skips no-ops and duplicates (A2);
  \item a gradient-boosted prior on proposal features (A3; \citealp{ke2017lightgbm});
  \item a tabular-foundation-model prior on the same features (TabPFN; \citealp{hollmann2025tabpfn});
  \item the manager's own zero-shot forecast (A4);
  \item small decision language models fine-tuned to output the 12-bin forecast (A5): Kev-0.8B and Kev-4B \citep{kev2026}, plus, as a control that isolates decision pre-training, the same fine-tuning applied to their Qwen3.5 base models \citep{qwen35};
  \item a learned execute-or-skip action head on the A5 representation (A6), compared with the closed-form rule applied to the same forecast.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
A1--A5 use the closed-form rules of Proposition~\ref{prop:onestep} and differ only in the forecast. A2 and the later arms add the rule layer, and A1 keeps knowledge-gradient verification. \paragraph{Offline gate and statistics.}
The run price is declared before any evaluation and is never tuned: $g=0.1\,\sigma_{\mathrm{node}}$ (primary), with $0.05$ and $0.2$ as sensitivities.

The primary offline quantity is an arm's held-out one-step value scaled to 20 logged proposals. In the sweep, every proposal is executed, so one proposal corresponds to one run. Skips are credited at $g$, and freed runs are not re-spent. Outside (NF) and (NI), this scaled one-step gain has no guaranteed sign relation to the campaign gain. It is a declared gate quantity, not an estimate of that gain. Offline evaluation of skipping is also optimistic for a Bayes myopic controller under (NF), and unbiased under (NI). The reason is that sweep histories contain results that a skipping controller would not have observed online. For learned forecasters the sign of this bias is undetermined.

The offline gate asks whether A3 or A5 exceeds A2 by the node's practical threshold $\delta_n$ on at least two nodes, with the lower 95\% bound above zero. The threshold is
\[
\delta_n=\max(0.002,\ 0.25\,\tilde s_n),
\]
where $\tilde s_n$ is the median paired standard deviation over the node's edits. Uncertainty comes from a cluster bootstrap over campaign seeds (2{,}000 draws); a seed's campaigns in both memory modes form one cluster.
