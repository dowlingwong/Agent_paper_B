\begin{abstract}
An automated research loop lets a language model propose changes to a training setup, runs each proposal, and keeps a candidate when its score beats the incumbent's. Scores are noisy and proposals repeat, so part of a fixed budget can go to duplicates and to keeps that do not hold up. We ask: at a matched budget of worker runs and manager calls, which control decisions raise a campaign's gain, re-measured on fresh seeds, and by how much? A control plane, separate from the proposer, decides whether to execute each proposal and whether to keep its result. On fresh campaigns we compare the default loop; the default loop with duplicates skipped; a rule that sets its run and keep thresholds from the measured noise and one fixed forecast per task; and the same rule with a learned forecast per proposal. The outcome is the improvement of the final configuration over the start, re-measured on fresh seeds. \LoopHeadline{} A separate, preregistered offline test asks whether a learned forecast improves one-step decisions on logged proposals: \GateVerdict. \end{abstract}
