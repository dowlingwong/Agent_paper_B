\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The closed loop.}
The headline result comes from seven controlled CPU nodes with single-parameter edits, one proposer, one memory mode, one budget ($W=\LoopW$) and one repetition. Six of the eight T0 nodes share one task template. The closed loop on code-level edits covers three GPU nodes and is descriptive. We make no claim about multi-step control (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}).

\paragraph{The offline gate.}
The offline one-step utility is a proxy for the closed-loop outcome, and the loops we run lie outside the conditions under which the two agree (Section~\ref{sec:theory}). The gate has low power (Section~\ref{sec:setup-gate}). A gate that does not pass is weak evidence against a gain from learning, and a gate that passes leaves the size of the gain uncertain. That the gate does not decide which arms run was fixed after two external readings of a draft and before any outcome existed (Table~\ref{tab:dev-nov}).

\paragraph{Theory.}
The closed forms assume Gaussian increments and noise and a fixed run price; hard budgets are not covered. The reduction theorem is conditional, and the actual loop lies outside it.
With a selected incumbent the deployed keep rule is Bayes-optimal only against an unselected record; after keeps it is conservative on most histories and can keep too many on some (Appendix~\ref{app:theory-scope}).

\paragraph{Nodes, proposers and labels.}
The GPU tier is small. Its nodes were not selected for noise: under the noise criterion used for T0 few of them would qualify, and each node's value is reported (Appendix~\ref{app:nodes}). One T0 node missed a liveness criterion and is a sensitivity row. The two sweeps differ in more than the proposer, and sweep~1 is not replayable call by call. The exam that selected the main proposer carries post-hoc amendments. Audit labels are means over five seeds and are themselves noisy. Duplicates receive no label. Jobs may share a GPU with other processes, and timing decides nothing; results that are compared with each other come from one hardware class (Appendix~\ref{app:hardware}).

\paragraph{What the outcome leaves open.}
\ifGatePassed
A passing gate supports a one-step gain of the passing arm on the nodes of the read and at one run price; its size is uncertain.
\else
With a gate that does not pass, the paper has no positive offline result on a learned forecast. The dispersion estimate that we report uses fitted forecasts, and a forecast that is too smooth makes it too small.
\fi
Decision-focused fine-tuning (A6) is trained only after a gate that passes and has no offline test read.
