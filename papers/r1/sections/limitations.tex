\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The gate quantity is a proxy.}
The offline one-step utility relies on decision independence as a proxy for closed-loop value (Section~\ref{sec:problem}). The loops we run lie outside the conditions under which the two agree. The closed loop that we report runs on the controlled tier only, with one repetition, and it includes learned arms only if the gate passes. We make no claim about multi-step control: the pilot that would have measured its value ended before its evidence stage.

\paragraph{Power.}
The preregistered gate has low power (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). A gate that does not pass is weak evidence against a gain from learning, and a gate that passes leaves the size of the gain uncertain.

\paragraph{Theory.}
The closed forms assume Gaussian increments and noise and a fixed run price. Hard budgets are not covered. The reduction theorem is conditional, and the shared error of the recorded incumbent places the actual loop outside it.

Under common random numbers with a selected incumbent, the comparison noise depends on the history. The deployed keep rule is then Bayes-optimal only against an unselected record about which the history carries no further information. After keeps, under the conditions of Appendix~\ref{app:theory}, which include a non-negative posterior mean of the record's error, it is conservative and keeps too few proposals. The same algebra holds under a noise component shared by the campaign, with the record's own fresh noise component in place of $(1-\varrho_n)e$. Its size on \texttt{digits\_mlp} is \CRNThresholdShiftDigits. The closed form of the record bias (Corollary~\ref{cor:bias-kernel}) assumes a noise kernel that need not hold after a keep; Proposition~\ref{prop:bias} does not. \TBD{Status of the author's proof check.}

\paragraph{Nodes and proposers.}
The controlled tier uses single-parameter edits on small CPU tasks, and six of its eight nodes share one task template. The GPU tier is small. One node missed a liveness criterion and is a sensitivity row. The two sweeps differ in more than the proposer, so cross-proposer contrasts confound the proposer with its serving stack. Sweep~1 is not replayable call by call. The main proposer was selected on exam data. Its selection rule was frozen before those data, but the exam carries post-hoc amendments (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}).

\paragraph{Labels.}
Audit labels are means over five seeds and are themselves noisy; the oracle of the gate is a noisy ceiling. Duplicates receive no label and are excluded from the learned arms' evaluation.

\paragraph{What the gate outcome leaves open.}
\ifGatePassed
A passing gate supports a gain of the passing arm on the nodes of the read and at one run price; the size of the gain is uncertain. Decision-focused fine-tuning (A6) has no offline test read, because it is trained after the gate, and it appears, if at all, only in the closed loop.
\else
With a gate that does not pass, the paper has no positive result on a learned forecast. Decision-focused fine-tuning (A6) is trained only after a gate that passes, so it was not trained, and the paper reports nothing about it. The dispersion bound that we report is an estimate from fitted forecasts, and a forecast that is too smooth makes it too small.
\fi
