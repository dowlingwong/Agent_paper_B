## Verdict: **REVISE**

The plan takes the comment's four-level ladder seriously, and several parts are good: the staged action space, the information principle, the matched-interaction design for online RL, the entry gates before LLM multi-step RL, the "not testable at this budget" exit, and the commitment to campaign-level fresh-seed evaluation. Most cited Paper B and Track S facts are accurate.

It cannot be adopted as the scope of a paper yet, for three reasons:

1. **The second confirmatory comparison (Q3) has no tier where it is both powered and has headroom** (BLOCKING B1). Training data and forks sit on E-T0, where the plan itself predicts a null. The tier where headroom is expected (E-INT) gets only a 100-instance test, with no in-tier training or tuning. The test is one-sided superiority, but a negative result is read as "a clean result".
2. **The method classification and the Q1/Q2 comparisons have confounds that the Track S history already exposed.** These are the level-shift wins, a belief model that is not held fixed, and a "multi-step" rule that classifies tuned static thresholds as multi-step and planning methods as not.
3. **The cost model rests on a latency for a proposer the plan does not use, and is internally inconsistent in four places** (SPT-2 components, SPT-3 arms and continuations, SPT-5 dev budget, SPT-6 policy count). As a result the "minimal version" timeline is not realistic.

All of these can be fixed at the plan stage, without new data.

**Counts:** 1 BLOCKING · 13 MAJOR · 16 MINOR.
