## Sizing scenario

As in the earlier report, if the current selection completes all **260 roots** (72 digits, 116 credit, 72 ionosphere), the observed source/cache allocated-size proxies and **40 retained audit copies per root**, plus the initial root copies, give **0.965 GiB**. Forty is an illustrative `2 × W` workload assumption, not a proven upper bound; W=20, llm_call_cap=80 and four audit seeds do not by themselves bound total log length, copied configurations or partial work.

For the remaining ledger, server, per-worker/audit outputs, editable and state-version archives, partial work and metadata, budget **100 times the largest observed two-ledger sweep-1 root** for each of the 260 roots: `300,387 × 100 × 260 = 7.274 GiB`. This is an explicitly chosen **100× sizing allowance**, approximately 28.6 MiB per root, not a measured or guaranteed bound on those extra items. Add the 0.965 GiB source/cache component: **8.24 GiB**. Round up to **10 GiB as a practical planning allowance** for these retained B1 outputs, provided the actual final workload is broadly comparable and monitoring below is performed. The allowance includes substantial headroom over the observed ledger samples, but is not an upper bound: in particular larger logs, more than 40 audit copies per root, state snapshot growth, unexpected node output, extra attempts or a different filesystem may exceed it. This does not reserve space, select a new protocol gate, or authorize a rerun of a STOP.

The reviewed 23:07 UTC inventory reported 613,359,476 KiB free (584.95 GiB) on the then-observed work/model filesystem, with 118,633,645 free inodes. Relative to the existing 100,000,000,000-byte floor, approximately **491.81 GiB** remained, or about **49 times the 10 GiB planning allowance** at that instant. The comparison does not prove the eventual output path is on that filesystem, that quota is visible or sufficient, that another job will not consume space, or that 20 workers fit in memory. Actual capacity must be rechecked against the final output locations, current other workloads, user quota and free inodes. Existing author-prescribed workers=20 remains unchanged.

During B1 retain the existing 100 GB runtime floor and operator checks at least every 30 minutes. Record actual free bytes/inodes, B1-owned output growth and current progress; use observed growth and time to stop to check projected headroom, and inspect more often if growth or contention rises. Any inability to continue safely follows the existing STOP and evidence-preservation paths. The allowance is not a new automatic threshold; it cannot guarantee against an unexpectedly large model response, rapid competing writes or a shutdown overshoot. If measurements show the 10 GiB scenario is unsuitable, revise the operational estimate from observed non-audit metadata rather than assuming the margin is a bound. Exact final HEAD, note/gate, handover and other launch conditions remain separate.
