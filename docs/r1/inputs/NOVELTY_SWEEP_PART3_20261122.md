## 2. Consequences

1. **C1 can no longer be presented as the first measurement of noise-driven false acceptance in autonomous experimentation.** Two workshop papers at NeurIPS 2026 measure it, one of them with the same meaningful-effect threshold. C1 stays, as supporting evidence under governance: fresh-seed audited ground truth, several nodes in two tiers, two proposers, duplicates and inert edits counted as wasted runs. Both workshop papers are cited as concurrent evidence of the phenomenon.
2. **The gate family is crowded** (PACE, SAGE, SGM and its descendants, the evidence-governed framework). All of them control a false-accept probability and decide after the candidate has been evaluated. None prices a run, screens before execution, or learns. The paper's stated differences (R1-C1-POSITION): a rule that maximises audited gain per run under a declared price; screening before a run is spent, from a per-proposal forecast; theory for a ledger-dependent proposer; a learned forecast against a theory-derived index policy at matched compute.
3. **Baselines.** An anytime-valid gate arm was already required (part 1, item 4). It is a reference arm in the T0 closed loop D.3a (R1-D3-MIN), charged for its replicate runs, next to the fixed-r replication + test baseline of R1-BX-D7.
4. **Decision-focused training.** Calibration Is Not Control is the closest precedent for training on decision utility instead of calibrated risk. The paper claims only the specific combination: a forecast of an audited increment distribution feeding a closed-form keep/skip rule under a run price.
5. **Endogenous proposer.** arXiv 2607.00871 names the endogenous proposal process as unproved for its gate. This supports presenting T4's results for a ledger-dependent proposer as a C2 contribution; read that paper in full before citing it.
6. **To read in full before citing specifics:** the five "Snippet" rows above; arXiv 2610.15738 (still not fetched since part 2).
