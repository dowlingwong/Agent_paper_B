## Exact blocker

* The candidate's resumed B1 runner reaches `ExamRunner.close()` (`r1/exam/runtime.py:750-755` at the baseline), which unlinks its test-owned `runner.lock`. Its campaign worker is a separate subprocess (`runtime.py:640-656`), with `worker_environment` overriding `PYTHONPATH`; it calls pinned harness `run_real_campaign` even in DRYRUN (`r1/exam/campaign.py`). The harness `_release_pending` attempts `guard.unlink()` and falls back to truncating it on an error (`autoresearch/control_plane/campaign.py:173-183` at author pin `a3fedbbd44309b3fdf99d853ad841b9ec2c299e2`); `_restore_editable_state` may unlink a file absent in the prior snapshot (`:899-906`). An earlier synthetic run logged 49 release events, but event logs do not distinguish unlink, fallback truncation, or already-absent guard. See `B1_SYNTHETIC_STAGEA_WORKER_REVIEW_20261107.md` on the unaccepted candidate branch. A parent-only Python patch cannot cover its spawned child, and simply denying unlink can cause the harness to truncate the guard.
* A unique pytest `--basetemp` alone does not preserve files: installed pytest 9.0.3's numbered `tmp_path` creation attempts to unlink a `current` symlink, and default FD capture uses `TemporaryFile`. Existing `B1_PYTEST_PRESERVATION_STATIC_REVIEW_20261107.md` and `B1_MAC_SANDBOX_SEMANTICS_REVIEW_20261107.md` describe the reviewed limits. The candidate requires safe fixture and capture selection plus coverage of its descendants and native dependencies; replacing one fixture or using `--capture=sys` does not prove that.
* A test-only move of an owned guard or lock to a unique non-overwriting `_to_delete/` destination could preserve its bytes and make the original name absent on normal release, but it would replace the real cleanup behavior in the test. Its use must be explicit and separately disclosed; it cannot establish production harness cleanup or real lease correctness. Merely checking `batch_state.json` before the move is racy: the parent can record a STOP after the check and before a child moves evidence. The current runner does not take a shared test coordination lock when it records STOP. A helper that accepts an arbitrary callback or observes state once cannot prove STOP preservation.

## Concrete unblock and ownership

An implementer must establish the required outcome: no unauthorized deletion, no alteration or resumption of a recorded STOP scene, correct test-owned path and process identity, coverage of the child processes, and faithful disclosure of any difference from production cleanup. One possible approach is an explicit **DRYRUN-only** parent/child coordination mechanism bound to a fresh test-owned root and exact path/inode identities; in that approach, every STOP publisher and retirement actor would need to participate in the same synchronization around STOP publication and retirement, and a process encountering a recorded STOP would refuse further retirement or resume. This is one approach requiring independent review, not the only possible solution; no such race is claimed to have occurred in a real B1 batch. If an adapter is chosen, it must be loaded in the actual campaign child as well as the runner, preserve a verifiable receipt and bytes for every retirement, refuse collisions or unowned/symlink paths, and remain unreachable from non-DRYRUN execution. Independently prove or enforce deletion-free behavior across the selected pytest/fixtures, fake server, native dependencies and child processes, including other unlink/truncate/replace paths; verify any signals target only a fresh test-owned process with stable identity. The adapter's altered cleanup and any incomplete process coverage must be stated in the test result. A fresh independent reviewer must inspect the exact implementation, fixture isolation and proof before a **new** uniquely rooted run; that run cannot reuse or change any prior STOP scene. Then the reviewer must check the resulting full fixture, including all intended Stage A and recovery assertions. Exact engineering time is **unknown**, and consists of multiple implementation and independent-review tasks; no duration is inferred from a mock-only check. Alternatively, only the author may change the no-deletion restriction or the pinned-harness decision; no such choice is assumed here.

Until those steps are completed, no replacement shim or synthetic runner should be used, and the former coverage remains STOP. This is separate from the real B1 launch blockers, including the unapproved note ordering, host ownership, real-worker determinism and final combined-checkout review.
