# M10 HK6 USR1 owned-unit checkpoint — candidate

**Fresh independent review required; not connected to a job or submission.** Runbook §1/§3(5) specifies batch-shell forwarding of USR1 to its own runner, archiving an open unit under `results/_interrupted/<jobid>/`, a subsequent whole-unit fresh copy and STOP after a second cut. `InterruptCheckpoint` is only an implementable cooperative **runner-side primitive**: its main-thread handler records USR1; after the caller has stopped/quiesced its own writers, the caller checks the flag and moves one owned open-unit directory on the same filesystem into an exclusively created numeric job-ID archive directory. On a failed attempt it retains the unit and any partial archive and refuses further attempts in that instance. It never starts a process, signals a child, retries, selects K or a failure policy, publishes a completed unit, or submits a job.

The runbook's complete job behavior remains unimplemented: shell trap and wait integration, timely interruption of real workers, verified quiescence, caller exit status, history/identity persistence and the cut-twice check, deadline handling, unit-complete publication, evidence/engineering separation, host and fake-Slurm tests. The caller must establish the archive root, actual job/unit identity, stable owned paths and a complete history, and must exit/report after a checkpoint or STOP; the flag is not a preemptive interrupt of a blocking worker. The archive method uses an exclusive job directory plus ordinary same-filesystem rename under the cooperative-process assumption, not an atomic adversarial no-replace guarantee or fsync/receipt durability guarantee. No claim is made that a scheduler's real signal, container, multiple threads, child process tree, cross-filesystem or remote filesystem is qualified.

The local standard-library tests ran **4 passed**, including a real self-owned synthetic subprocess sent USR1, preserving exact partial bytes and inode under the new job directory; other cases test absent signal, existing archive collision, symlink refusal, injected rename failure and refusal to retry that failed archive. The subprocess had a bounded own timeout and only that child could be killed in test cleanup; all files were internal test temporaries, within the author's exception. A second test run after adding the failed-attempt latch also gave **4 passed**. Both files parse with Python 3.9 grammar; execution used the locally available system Python, not an independently verified 3.9 runtime. No HoreKa, Slurm, model, GPU, real job or B1 output was accessed. Existing EVIDENCE STOP, author decisions and default-off submission remain unchanged.
