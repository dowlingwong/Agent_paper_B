## 2. Reading

1. **(b2) qualifies because of its +∞ convention, as in D8-SKEW.** D8-SKEW's post hoc diagnostic (§3.3) found that
   the screen decides most reps. Under that convention, any resample with a nonpositive eligible-node denominator gets
   t\* = +∞, so the arm fails whenever more than 2.5 % of resamples have one. Without the screen, the studentized
   interval was not calibrated under left skew there. The settings here (V1n, z ≥ 3, P3) are the same in kind. So
   (b2) is in effect "fail unless the denominators are reliably positive, then a studentized IQM bound".
   - It is the only rule that meets the author's criterion.
   - Its cost is power: 0.36 mean two-arm against 0.84–0.90 for the numerator tests. At μ = 1 it is almost never
     passed (0.05).
2. **(c″J), Johnson's skew correction, comes closest among the numerator tests.**
   - It exceeds 0.035 per arm in 3 of the 24 T1 × 12 scenarios, all under left skew, at 0.0375–0.0425. That is less
     than one MC SE above the threshold.
   - On T1 × 6 it exceeds in 1 scenario (0.040).
   - Its power is 0.84.
   - It does not qualify under the declared criterion. A narrow miss is still a miss.
3. **The bootstrap-t (c′) roughly halves (c)'s excess,** but does not remove it (max 0.050).
4. **The stratified signed-rank test (c″SR) fails badly under left skew,** up to 0.685 per arm, as declared in
   advance. Its null is symmetry, not a zero mean, and the median gain under left skew is positive.
5. **The left-skewed case is extreme.** The noise's skewness is about −6.2, and the arms' about −4.4. All the
   exceedances come from it.

  with the declared convention that a resample with a nonpositive eligible-node denominator, a zero se\* or a
  non-finite t\* counts as +∞. The PoI conjunct is kept.
