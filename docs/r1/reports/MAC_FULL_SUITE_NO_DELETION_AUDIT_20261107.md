## Conclusion

## Practical path

1. Inventory direct and indirect deletes and process signalling for the exact suite and dependency versions, including Python subprocesses, shell utilities, fixture finalizers, pytest cleanup, and run-specific fake-server paths. Avoid signalling processes whose ownership and start identity cannot be proven. Do not reuse recorded STOPs.
2. In a separately reviewed test-only adaptation, create cases needing an absent fixture in that state from the start, or move newly created synthetic entries into a unique non-overwriting `_to_delete/` location. Separately address application cleanup exercised by tests, `TemporaryDirectory`, pytest, and child processes. If production cleanup must change, identify the runtime consequences and obtain the required independent review; a test-only preservation mechanism must be shown to cover subprocesses without changing the behavior under test. A Python monkeypatch alone does not demonstrate coverage of shell tools, alternate interpreters, or subprocesses; a deny-only policy can turn intended absence tests into different behavior or silently suppress cleanup errors.
3. Each eventual attempt should use a never-before-existing `--basetemp` and a separate never-before-existing `TMPDIR`, with shared pytest cache disabled and bytecode writes disabled. These measures protect existing roots and avoid the known shared-retention risk; they are necessary but are not a no-deletion proof. Preserve complete new STOP evidence and do not resume a STOP. Validate the reviewed no-deletion enforcement across subprocesses before calling the suite safe.

A bounded subset cannot be certified from path searches alone: even tests without visible removal may call cleanup indirectly. Select individual tests only after tracing their fixtures and subprocesses and proving that they cannot remove data or affect unrelated processes. The time and engineering effort for complete subprocess coverage are not yet established.
