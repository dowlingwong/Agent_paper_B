## 1. What was compared

The model is that of D8-TEST, with these settings:
- V1n test side and R1 eligibility (train + dev, z ≥ 3);
- splits P2 and P3, and the T1 × 12 layout, for every condition;
- T1 × 6 for normal data with homogeneous μ only;
- two arms whose noise is correlated 0.5.

On T1 × 12 a node has 4 (T1, P2), 6 (T1, P3; T0, P2) or 9 (T0, P3) test instances.

There are six conditions on T1 × 12:
- noise: normal, left-skewed or t3 (scaled to sd 1). The left-skewed noise is a standardised −lognormal(0, 1). The
  noise e has skewness ≈ −6.2. Each arm's noise is the average of two such draws, so its skewness is ≈ −4.4, and at
  g = 0 an arm has a > 0 with probability ≈ 0.655.
- per-node μ: homogeneous, or heterogeneous with μ_j = μ·U(0.5, 1.5).

The rules (declaration §2) all keep the gate's PoI conjunct:
- **(a)** IQM of the per-node ratio of means, percentile bootstrap: the current rule;
- **(b1)** the same IQM, BCa interval;
- **(b2)** the same IQM, studentized bootstrap. A resample in which any eligible node has mean(d\*) ≤ 0 gets
  t\* = +∞; this was declared as the conservative convention.
- **(c)** a numerator test.
  - The request asks for "the mean of a over eligible nodes' test instances". The declaration reads this as the
    node-equal mean of the per-node means of a, with a node-stratified variance and a Welch–Satterthwaite Student-t
    reference distribution.
  - **Flag for the author.** "The PLAN's Student-t" (R1-BX-REVISIONS (c), E5) concerns soft labels in the PLAN. The
    declaration takes it as the reference distribution of this test.
  - The IQM was declared to be reported as an effect size. The script does not output it per rule; (c)'s effect size
    would be the same IQM as for (a), and the omission is noted here.
- **(d)** V2's estimand, the tier-pooled denominator, with a point STOP.

**Declared decision rule (§4).** A rule is calibrated if, in every scenario of every T1 × 12 condition, it passes
falsely at g = 0 in at most 0.035 of reps per arm and at most 0.065 for two arms (Holm). Among the calibrated rules,
the one with the highest mean two-arm power at g = 0.3 over μ ≥ 1 is recommended.

**Deviation from the declaration.** The declaration lists the BCa failure conditions. The script adds one: the arm
also fails when 1 − a·(z0 + z_α) ≤ 0, because the adjusted quantile is then undefined. Counting it as a fail is
conservative. The reported BCa failure share counts reps in which either arm failed.

## 3. Reading

### 3.1 Under left skew, every rule except (b2) fails, worse at larger μ

The excess grows with μ mainly because the V1n test-side STOP rate falls: at P2 it is 0.62 at μ = 0.5 and 0.08 at
μ = 2. The rate of reps with no eligible node is 0 throughout. The rules, from worst to least bad:
- **(d) and (a).** Both take an IQM of skewed per-node estimates, and with 4–9 test instances that IQM is biased
  upward at g = 0. (d)'s pooled denominator adds no protection.
- **(b1) BCa.** Under left skew it has about half of (a)'s false-pass rate, but it still exceeds the thresholds. It
  also fails to compute in up to 54 % of read reps on T1 × 12 (58 % on T1 × 6), mostly because a jackknife
  denominator is ≤ 0, and the declaration counts those reps as fails.
- **(c) numerator t-test.** It is the closest, at most 0.083 per arm. A one-sided t-test is anti-conservative in its
  upper tail under left-skewed data: samples without an extreme negative value have both a high mean and a low
  variance.

### 3.2 The PoI conjunct: no protection under skew, and decisive under symmetric noise

- **Under left skew.** At g = 0 each arm has a > 0 with probability ≈ 0.655, so the PoI bound passes. The
  primary-bound-only rates are within 0.005 of the full rates.
- **Under normal and t3 noise.** The PoI null (0.5) is exactly true at g = 0, so the PoI conjunct removes many false
  passes. The primary bound alone exceeds the thresholds for three rules. Maxima over the 8 T1 × 12 scenarios of each
  condition, per arm / two-arm:

  | Rule | normal hom | normal het | t3 hom | t3 het | T1 × 6 (normal hom) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | (a) | 0.030 / **0.068** | 0.033 / 0.050 | **0.038** / 0.050 | 0.035 / 0.053 | **0.050** / **0.080** |
  | (b1) | **0.053** / **0.105** | **0.053** / **0.085** | **0.070** / **0.135** | **0.075** / **0.120** | **0.068** / **0.118** |
  | (b2) | 0.030 / 0.055 | 0.023 / 0.038 | 0.023 / 0.045 | 0.018 / 0.028 | 0.025 / 0.045 |
  | (c) | 0.030 / 0.055 | **0.040** / 0.050 | **0.038** / 0.053 | 0.030 / 0.048 | 0.018 / 0.040 |
  | (d) | **0.040** / **0.073** | **0.043** / **0.068** | **0.053** / **0.070** | **0.050** / **0.070** | **0.075** / **0.135** |

  Bold values are over 0.035 per arm or 0.065 two-arm. (Within MC error: (a) at 0.038 or 0.068; (c) at 0.038.)
- So under symmetric noise:
  - the calibration of (b1) and (d), and partly of (a), depends on the PoI conjunct;
  - (b1)'s primary bound is worse than (a)'s;
  - (c)'s primary bound is close to nominal, and (b2)'s is within the thresholds.
- PoI protects only when the noise is symmetric, so it cannot be relied on in general.

### 3.3 (b2) is calibrated because of its +∞ convention, not because studentizing fixes the skew

This is the post hoc diagnostic (`b2_diag.md`, homogeneous μ, T1 × 12, P2 and P3, all μ). It is a diagnostic, not a
rule.

**How the convention acts.** With 2000 resamples, the declared convention fails the arm automatically whenever more
than 2.5 % of resamples contain an eligible node with mean(d\*) ≤ 0. In effect, (b2) as declared is a
denominator-positivity screen.

**The screen decides most read reps.** It makes the decision in this share of read reps:
- left-skewed, g = 0: 0.59–0.95;
- normal, g = 0.3: 0.81–0.96 at μ ≤ 1, and 0.03–0.20 at μ = 2.

**Dropping the bad resamples (variant drop-bad).** The diagnostic drops those resamples instead of giving them +∞,
with the PoI conjunct kept:
- **Left skew, g = 0.** Variant drop-bad has a false-pass rate of 0.048–0.090 per arm and 0.085–0.152 two-arm. That
  is no better than (c) or (b1).
- **Normal noise, g = 0.** It stays within the thresholds (≤ 0.020 per arm and ≤ 0.033 two-arm).
- **Power.** Its power is close to that of (c). At normal noise, g = 0.3, μ = 1, two-arm power is 0.44 at P2 and
  0.61 at P3, against 0.01 and 0.06 as declared.

The declared (b2) rows of the diagnostic agree with `sim_output.json` within MC error.

So the studentized interval itself is not shown to be calibrated under left skew. The calibration that the declared
rule finds belongs to the screen, and the screen is also why (b2) has almost no power at μ ≤ 1.

### 3.4 The skew case is extreme

The arm noise has skewness ≈ −4.4. Milder skew was not simulated, so how fast the excess shrinks as the skew
decreases is not known from this run.

## 4. Recommendation (for the author to decide)

- **By the declared decision rule: (b2), the studentized bootstrap of the gap-closed IQM, with V1n and R1 z ≥ 3.**
  - It is the only rule that keeps both false-pass thresholds in all six conditions.
  - Its cost is a large loss of power: mean two-arm power is 0.29 against 0.84 for (c), and near zero at μ = 1.
  - What calibrates it is the resample screen ("fail unless at most 2.5 % of resamples keep every eligible node
    denominator positive"), not the studentization (§3.3).
- **Routes the author may prefer.** Nothing here changes the declared rule. Each of these routes is post hoc
  relative to this unit and needs its own declaration before any run:
  - **adopt (b2)** as declared, understanding it as a screen, and accept the power;
  - **adopt (c)**. Under extreme left skew it passes falsely up to 0.083 per arm and 0.12 two-arm. Under normal, t3
    and heterogeneous μ it is calibrated, and its primary bound is close to nominal without help from PoI. Disclose
    that.
  - **run a further declared unit**. Candidates are (b2) without the +∞ convention, a studentized or bootstrap-t
    version of (c), and milder skew levels.
- **Whatever is chosen:**
  - PoI gives no protection against skew. Under symmetric noise it is what keeps (b1) and (d) calibrated (§3.2). The
    confirmation draft should say both.
  - The strongest evidence against (d) and (b1) is (d)'s left-skew false-pass rate and (b1)'s primary-bound excess
    under symmetric noise. (d)'s T1 × 6 exceedance is within MC error.

## 5. Limits

- g is common to all nodes, so under homogeneous μ the per-node and pooled estimands coincide. Heterogeneous g is not
  simulated.
- Under heterogeneous μ, (d)'s statistic, the IQM of mean(a_j)/D_T, is not exactly g at g = 0.3. Its power there is
  for a slightly different quantity. False-pass at g = 0 is unaffected.
- δ_n is non-binding.
- The arm-noise correlation of 0.5 is an assumption.
- The two-arm rate is "either arm passes at 97.5 %", which is what the gate's Holm step-down passes on.
- (b2)'s se is an approximation (declaration §2).
- The diagnostic of §3.3 covers homogeneous μ only.
