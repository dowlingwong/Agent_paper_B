## 1. What was compared

The model is that of D8-TEST, with these settings:
- V1n test side and R1 eligibility (train + dev, z ≥ 3);
- splits P2 and P3, and the T1 × 12 layout, for every condition;
- T1 × 6 for normal data with homogeneous μ only;
- two arms whose noise is correlated 0.5.

There are six conditions on T1 × 12:
- noise: normal, left-skewed (standardised −lognormal(0, 1), skewness ≈ −6.2) or t3 (scaled to sd 1);
- per-node μ: homogeneous, or heterogeneous with μ_j = μ·U(0.5, 1.5).

The rules (declaration §2) all keep the gate's PoI conjunct:
- **(a)** IQM of the per-node ratio of means, percentile bootstrap: the current rule;
- **(b1)** the same IQM, BCa interval;
- **(b2)** the same IQM, studentized bootstrap;
- **(c)** a numerator test: node-equal mean of a over the eligible nodes, Welch–Satterthwaite Student-t. The IQM is
  an effect size only.
- **(d)** V2's estimand, the tier-pooled denominator, with a point STOP.

**Declared decision rule (§4).** A rule is calibrated if, in every scenario of every T1 × 12 condition, it passes
falsely at g = 0 in at most 0.035 of reps per arm and at most 0.065 for two arms (Holm). Among the calibrated rules,
the one with the highest mean two-arm power at g = 0.3 over μ ≥ 1 is recommended.

## 3. Reading

1. **The calibration problem is specific to left skew.** Under t3 heavy tails and under heterogeneous μ, every rule is
   calibrated. Under the left-skewed condition, every rule except (b2) fails, and the failure grows with μ, because
   more nodes become eligible. The order, from worst to least bad:
   - **(d) and (a).** Both take an IQM of skewed per-node estimates, and with 6–9 test instances that IQM is biased
     upward at g = 0. (d)'s pooled denominator adds no protection.
   - **(b1) BCa.** It halves (a)'s excess, but it cannot remove it. It also fails to compute in up to 58 % of read
     reps at low μ (a jackknife denominator ≤ 0), and the declaration counts those reps as fails.
   - **(c) numerator t-test.** It is closest, at most 0.083 per arm. The upper tail of a one-sided t-test is
     anti-conservative for left-skewed data: samples without an extreme negative value have both a high mean and a
     low variance.
2. **The PoI conjunct does not protect under skew.** At g = 0 the true PoI is above 0.5, because the median gain is
   positive. Its bound passes, so the primary-bound-only rates equal the full rates.
3. **(b2) is calibrated because it is very conservative, and its power is low.**
   - Two-arm power at μ = 1 is 0.01 (P2) and 0.09 (P3) on normal homogeneous data. (c) has 0.51 and 0.72 there.
   - Over μ ≥ 1 its mean two-arm power is 0.14–0.43 by condition. The other rules have 0.63–0.98.
   - The likely reasons: every resample with a node denominator ≤ 0 gets t\* = +∞ (declared as conservative); its
     delta-method se ignores the trimming; and the studentized interval widens with the skewed ratio's variance.
4. **The skew case is extreme.** The standardised lognormal(0, 1) has skewness about −6.2. Milder skew was not
   simulated, so how fast the excess shrinks as the skew decreases is not known from this run.

## 4. Recommendation (for the author to decide)

- **By the declared decision rule: (b2), the studentized bootstrap of the gap-closed IQM, with V1n and R1 z ≥ 3.** It
  is the only rule that keeps both false-pass thresholds in all six conditions. Its cost is a large loss of power:
  mean two-arm power 0.29 against 0.84 for (c), and near zero at μ = 1.
- Nothing here changes the declared rule. But the trade-off is stark, so the author may prefer another route. Each of
  these is post hoc relative to this unit and would need its own declaration before any run:
  - **adopt (b2)** and accept the power;
  - **adopt (c)** and accept a false-pass rate of up to 0.083 per arm and 0.12 two-arm under extreme left skew, while
    staying calibrated under normal, t3 and heterogeneous μ. Disclose that.
  - **run a further declared unit,** for example: a studentized or bootstrap-t version of the numerator test (c);
    (b2) without the +∞ convention; and milder skew levels.
- **Whatever is chosen:**
  - the PoI conjunct gives no protection against skew, and the confirmation draft should say so;
  - the T1 × 6 exceedance of (d) and BCa's failure-to-compute rate argue against (d) and (b1).
