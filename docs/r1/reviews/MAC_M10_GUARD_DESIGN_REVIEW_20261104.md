## Verdict: not accepted, two major design clarifications

**MAJOR 1 — Scope the evidence gate to the closed loop, not just HK6/HK7.** §2 singles out HK6/HK7 for the committed author authorization; the runbook's opening explicitly defines EVIDENCE as the T1 sweep, its audits **and the closed loop**. A reviewer or implementer could otherwise read the phase-specific bullet as the complete list. Specify an independently reviewed mapping covering every evidence task, including closed-loop jobs regardless of phase or J3 template, and refuse any evidence task without the relevant committed author note. Distinguish engineering J3 jobs from evidence J3 jobs. Add a negative test for a closed-loop J3 command declared as engineering. R1-HOREKA-J3-CONFIRM resolves the prior question about J3 under the submission ruling; it is not an evidence authorization.

**MAJOR 2 — Hash-check-at-start is not enough to establish bytes actually executed.** §2 allows a committed checkout to be validated, including executable links and untracked code, and says to check bytes before submitting and inside the job before starting the entrypoint. A mutable file, symlink target, interpreter, dynamically imported module, wrapper or input can change after validation and before or during use. Clarify that the execution environment and all behavior-relevant dependencies must remain immutable for the period of use, with verifiable enforcement; otherwise submission or execution refuses. A pinned read-only artifact is one possible proposed mechanism but needs implementation and site-specific review, including symlinks and dynamic dependencies. J0's pinned downloads/build inputs require a defined bootstrap boundary rather than an assumption that a finished runtime already exists. Add adversarial tests for replacement after verification as well as before it. No hash scheme should be described as proving semantic classification without an independent review.

## Minor clarification

The runbook guards the user's jobs (`squeue -u`, `sacct -u`), while the draft uses “account-wide” for both admission and accounting. An account can potentially cover multiple users. State the required population precisely: the runbook minimum is all of this user's relevant jobs, including unrelated jobs and the queue probe; wider account-level admission/accounting may be a conservative proposed site control. Verify the actual user/account/QoS limit scopes with the site. Do not assert account-wide counts, admin capabilities, a watermark, or the feasibility of an atomic gateway without evidence. If these cannot be established, keep the refusal.

## Checks and limits

The proposed design correctly separates a bundle's reviewed identity, the submission ruling, evidence authorization, and queue/budget controls. Its reading of the PLAN row matches the decision and proposed recorder's consequence; the NHR answer remains pending, and a restrictive reply remains a STOP. Both proposed admission paths explicitly require enforcement of exclusivity and proof of accounting completeness, rather than relying on an advisory lock, a zero from ordinary queries, or an incomplete local journal. Ambiguous submissions retain a conservative reservation and block further admissions pending reconciliation. The proposed tests address concurrency, unknown history, idempotency, crash boundaries, time guards and dirty content. These are requirements for future review, not demonstrated capabilities or authorization to conduct HK1.

No implementation, deployment or operational workflow is accepted by this review; each needs a separate frozen-byte independent review, the necessary site facts and the applicable author decisions. The restrictive site-control design may demand additional administrative or author action and must not be represented as a requirement already adopted in PLAN.
