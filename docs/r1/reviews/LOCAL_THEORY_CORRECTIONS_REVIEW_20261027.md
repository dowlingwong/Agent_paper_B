## Re-review of commit 183ebe9

The revision addresses the four findings below sufficiently to send the proposal to the author for proof checking. The original rejection and findings are retained as the review history; they do not describe the current verdict.

1. **Continuation condition — addressed.** Proposal:27 now states the two distinct equalities: E2 continuation invariance conditional on the observed execution information across feasible keep decisions, and equality of the expected post-execution continuation with skip's continuation using each branch's actual information. With those equalities at all relevant histories, the backward-induction comparison is valid: at E2 the common future term cancels, and at E1 the two continuation terms cancel. This is a strong value assumption, not a demonstration that the real proposer satisfies it. The author must formalize the filtration, interventions, policy classes, conditional laws and existence or use of suprema before adopting a theorem.
2. **Screening domain — addressed.** Proposal:25 now excludes duplicates, states the no-op observation and conditional Gaussian assumptions, handles π=1 and τ=0, identifies feasible actions and equality ties, and explains when a nontrivial mixture needs a coarser information set. The τ=0 case is a separate extension to the τ>0 formula. The formula is an immediate comparison at the same supported history.
3. **Campaign gain — addressed.** Proposal:27 now requires proof of the policy-specific conditional reward marginals under a coupling or an appropriate performance identity, and otherwise retains only the one-step gain. It does not assert that the required coupling exists.
4. **Verification assumptions — addressed.** Proposal:13,19 now identifies the independent Gaussian construction and fixed split reuse, positive integer r, v, the Bayes baseline and updated response, the externally fixed run price, cost and feasibility. The one-step comparison is subject to its stated absence of continuation effects. The frozen response is not equated with the Bayes response.

The threshold, zero-noise and VoI checks below remain applicable. At q=0 all m tie, and at Δ=0 the sign-error convention remains an author-check item. The author must choose Option A or make Option B fully formal and prove its assumptions and backward induction; acceptance here is not acceptance of those assumptions for any empirical system. The proposal does not establish hard-budget optimality, a feedback effect, or a campaign-gain identity without its stated additional proof.

## Original findings before revision

1. **Global Option B does not yet give a checkable sufficient condition.** The proposal says “policy-uniform equality of optimal continuation values under interventions on the current feasible execute/skip and keep decisions at the actual information set” and then asserts backward induction (proposal:27,33). It does not define the intervention, timing, sigma-fields, or whether equality holds conditional on the observed execution result and for measurable, signal-dependent keep rules. Equality averaged at the pre-execution history for each fixed keep action is insufficient: if a fair signal H takes values ±1 and future reward is kH, both fixed k=0 and k=1 have zero expected continuation before H, but choosing k=1{H=1} yields 1/2. This is an illustrative decision problem, not a claim that it satisfies the Gaussian source model. **Action:** specify a finite-horizon controlled process with integrable rewards, feasible non-anticipating policies, and regular conditional kernels. A sufficient formulation requires, almost surely at each reached pre-execution history, that (i) after execution, optimal future value conditional on the actual observed H is independent of every feasible keep/discard intervention, and (ii) the expectation of that common post-execution continuation equals the optimal continuation under skip at the pre-execution information set. Include all observations revealed by execution and the actual available information in the policy classes; do not silently give the skip branch H. Establish these equalities for the relevant interventions and all histories/policies needed for induction. Then show E2 maximizes immediate conditional reward and E1 compares V*−g with zero. A stronger common-information/interventional kernel condition could instead be stated and proved to imply these two equalities. This addresses the information asymmetry identified in the audit (audit:43–47,53–55; T4 §§1,6). Until then, retain Option A and do not assert global DI optimality.

2. **Specify the domain and all assumptions for screening.** Option A invokes a “corrected Proposition 6” without stating its content (proposal:25); T4 explicitly excludes duplicates and treats them separately (T4 §1 and §4, Corollary 8a). State that the Gaussian component and comparison kernel apply conditionally on the actual pre-execution history and non-no-op, non-duplicate event; state the no-op yields Δ=H=0, the relevant nondegeneracy and the policy's feasible action set. Handle π=1 directly with V*=0, and τ=0 with its known-mean action rather than an undefined threshold. If no-op status is already measurable from the full history, π is 0 or 1 almost surely; a mixture requires a specified coarser information set (audit:35–37). All conditional statements should be almost sure on supported histories, with integrability and defined tie handling. Under these conditions the one-step comparison and same-history gain in proposal:25 are correct; equality permits either action, so phrase the rule as execute for V*>g, skip for V*<g, and either at equality.

3. **Define the common history law before asserting a campaign gain.** Proposal:27 correctly flags this requirement. It is not enough merely to name a distribution or coupling: prove that under it the two policies' conditional immediate rewards have the claimed marginals at every stage, so linearity yields the sum, or derive the difference using a valid policy-specific performance identity. Execute/skip and keep can alter future incumbents, observations, proposals and feasibility (T4 §§1,4,6; audit:43–47). Without that proof the correct conclusion is the conditional one-step gain (g−V*)⁺, not an equality for trajectory returns.

4. **Make verification assumptions and baseline explicit.** Proposal:13,19 refers to a “stated joint Gaussian model” but does not restate the joint construction, defines neither v in the bound nor the reference policy for VoI. Define v=Var(Δ|H), r as a positive integer, and independent Gaussian Δ, campaign seed errors, and fresh replicate errors, preserving the fixed-policy reuse of the campaign split error and independent fresh draws across replicates (T2 §1; audit:5,15). Define VoI relative to the best Bayes keep/discard action using H alone, with the updated Bayes action using both H and the verification data; apply conditional statements almost surely. Nested information suffices for nondecreasing Bayes VoI, and the sample mean is sufficient for the specified exchangeable Gaussian fixed/vary models. The price comparison assumes exactly 2r additional runs, an additive externally fixed g per run, feasibility, and no other continuation effect. The frozen D̄>0 response is a different decision rule and does not automatically realize this VoI (T2:15–16,37–51; audit:29–33). Equality in the index is a tie, with no verification under the proposed strict rule.

## Mathematical checks

- **Proposition 1:** With independent Gaussian Δ and ε and τ,σ>0, E[Δ|H=h]=μ+τ²(h−μ)/(τ²+σ²); its zero is −μσ²/τ², and the threshold value and zero-threshold iff μ=0 claims in proposal:7 follow. At σ=0, H=Δ and accepting any negative interval strictly loses value; zero is the unique finite optimal threshold for τ>0, regardless of μ. At τ=0, keep always for μ>0, discard always for μ<0, and all decisions tie for μ=0. The stated fixed-increment sign error applies to Δ≠0 and σ>0; Δ=0 requires a declared sign/tie convention, and σ=0 gives zero error for nonzero Δ. The original contrary edge statement is at T2:27; see audit:7–11.
- **Proposition 2:** Conditional Gaussianity gives M=E[Δ|H,D̄] ∼ N(m,q²) given H. Thus E[M⁺|H]−m⁺ equals the proposed q[φ(|m|/q)−(|m|/q)Φ(−|m|/q)], and tends to zero at q=0. For fixed q>0, the derivative in |m| is −Φ(−|m|/q), and the derivative in q is φ(|m|/q)>0. More nested observations cannot decrease Bayes value. The proposal correctly removes general diminishing returns; the explicit counterexample is audit:13–17. The maximum-at-zero statement is meaningful across m at fixed q; at q=0 all m tie.
- **Proposition 5:** VoI≤qφ(0)≤√vφ(0) follows from the previous expression and total variance. The priced one-step index therefore cannot strictly prefer verify when 2rg≥√vφ(0). This is neither a hard-budget optimum nor proof of value under the frozen response (proposal:19; T2:92–100; audit:29–33).
- **Information and causal interpretation:** Observational equality conditional on H after execution is not an interventional equality at skip, where H may be unavailable. Signal acquisition can improve future decisions even when a future law conditional on H is action invariant (T4 §§1,6; audit:43–47,53–55). A prospective audited-increment endpoint may inform an empirical question but does not establish the policy-uniform assumption or a feedback effect (proposal:35).

The author's choice between the one-step and global formulations, and an independent author proof check, remain outstanding (proposal:37).
