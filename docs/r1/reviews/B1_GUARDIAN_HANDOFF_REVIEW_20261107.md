## What changes

In the originally identified combined-review finding 3, the guardian exited immediately after `FINISH`, before the supervisor's publication. The candidate now unregisters the runner witness after an empty-subtree check, acknowledges `FINISHED`, and retains the inherited lease while polling the supervisor pidfd, supervisor STOP channel and the new handoff socket (`r1/exam/b1_cgroup_guardian.py:84–116,142–161`). The supervisor waits for the runner and checks finalization before sending `RELEASE` (`r1/scripts/b1_supervisor.py:135–150`). Thus, **for the particular ordering where the guardian remains alive, the supervisor dies after FINISH and before RELEASE, and pidfd polling works**, the guardian detects that death and retains the lease. This is a narrower source-level improvement, not proof that all finalization deaths are safe. The mocked simultaneous-event test covers a death already present in the same `poll` result; it cannot establish atomicity after that snapshot.

## Blocking findings

1. **A reader can see success when release has failed and STOP is not recorded.** `_publish_or_reconcile` can make the waited-success receipt verifier-valid at supervisor lines 64–75 and 145–146, before `sendall(RELEASE)` and the acknowledgement check at lines 148–150. A send/receive timeout, error, or bad acknowledgement then enters `_retain_on_stop` at lines 153–154; its STOP write is explicitly best-effort (lines 38–50). If that write fails, the already published receipt can still satisfy the reader. The candidate report recognizes this defect. This violates the requested distinction between a completed publication and a reported STOP; it is not a claim that a real occurrence was observed. **Unblock:** implement one reviewed completion/STOP protocol with a precise commit point and reader rules covering publication, release/ack failure and failed STOP persistence; exercise the actual ordering with failure injection. **Owner:** implementer and independent reviewer; **effort:** unknown until protocol is chosen, then site qualification separately.

2. **The death priority is only a poll snapshot.** At guardian lines 87–98 a supervisor death present in the returned event set wins, but after that check the guardian may receive RELEASE, send `RELEASED`, and return at lines 105–116 without another supervisor liveness check. If the supervisor dies after the event snapshot and after or concurrent with receipt of the acknowledgement, the guardian may exit and the last lease reference can disappear, even though the supervisor did not complete its normal return. Rechecking cannot by itself remove a check-then-death race. The candidate already calls out the release/publication boundary, but the simultaneous mock does not prove death always wins. **Unblock:** specify the exact successful commit point and what supervisor death after that point means for acceptance and lease handling; implement and test the whole lifecycle against that contract, including after-poll death. **Owner:** implementer and reviewer; **effort:** unknown, coupled to finding 1.

3. **Guardian failure after the runner's point check is not independently detected before publication.** `B1Containment.finish()` checks `guardian.poll()` only immediately after FINISHED (`r1/exam/b1_cgroup.py:512–523`). The guardian may die subsequently; the runner can still write finalization and exit, and the supervisor can publish before its later release send/receive exposes the loss. If the supervisor also dies in that interval no guardian remains to retain its lease. The receipt publication-before-ack issue makes the failure visible to readers too. **Unblock:** incorporate guardian lifecycle/ack failure into the reviewed protocol and test death after FINISHED, after runner exit, and across release; site operator must separately qualify the actual inherited descriptor and cgroup behavior. **Owner:** implementer/reviewer, then authorized operator; **effort:** unknown.

The supervisor still holds its own lease while it is alive and enters `_retain_on_stop` on catchable post-publication errors, so the above do not assert that every such error immediately releases the lock. Likewise an empty cgroup at FINISH means the old finding did not itself prove a surviving worker or GPU process. No conclusion is drawn about unrelated strict-no-deletion harness paths, the author gate or overall B1 readiness. Preserve this candidate as development work; do not use it to launch B1 or claim the combined review is closed.
