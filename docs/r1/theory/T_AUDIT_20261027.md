## Proposition 1 — **Wrong** (edge-case claim in the proof); main formula correct with added assumptions

Claim: for Δ~N(μ,τ²), H=Δ+noise with variance σ_d², threshold t has value μΦ(−z)+(τ²/s)φ(z), and t*=−μσ_d²/τ² is Bayes-optimal; zero threshold is optimal iff μ=0, with fixed-increment error Φ(−|Δ|/σ_d) (T2:11–23). With independent normal signal/noise, τ>0 and σ_d>0, E[Δ|H=h]=μ+(τ²/s²)(h−μ), and E[Δ 1{H>t}]=μP(H>t)+(τ²/s)φ(z); differentiating gives −f_H(t)E[Δ|H=t], establishing the unique threshold and iff assertion. Error formula assumes Δ≠0 and a specified treatment of Δ=0; under strict keep, Δ=0 has no decision-value loss and sign-error conventions change the probability.

The edge assertion in T2:25 is false: when σ_d=0 and τ>0, t*=0 is the **unique finite optimal threshold**, not every t≤0. For example μ=0, τ=1, t=−1 accepts negative Δ on (−1,0), strictly reducing value. Here 0 is optimal for every μ, so the iff claim does require σ_d>0. For τ=0 the displayed t* is undefined: known μ>0 means always keep, μ<0 always discard, μ=0 all choices tie; s=0 additionally makes z undefined. At Δ=0, σ_d=0 the displayed error rate is 0/0.

## Proposition 2 — **Wrong** (diminishing returns as stated)

Claim: conditional Bayes VoI is qψ(|m|/q), peaks at m=0, decreases with |m| and has diminishing returns in r (T2:35–42). With joint Gaussianity and q²=Var(E[Δ|H,D̄]|H)>0, the truncated-normal positive-part expectation gives the formula; its derivative in |m| is −Φ(−|m|/q), and in q is φ(|m|/q)>0. At q=0 define VoI=0 by continuity. Under vary-policy independent replications, v=Var(Δ|H)>0 and q_r²=v−(1/v+r/σ_d²)⁻¹; the fixed-policy analogue is also nondecreasing under independent new training-seed draws. The location and distance claims require τ>0 and nondegenerate signal; when q=0 all observations tie.

T2:46 proves concavity of q_r², **not** concavity of VoI(q_r): VoI is convex in q for nonzero m, since d²VoI/dq²=(m²/q³)φ(m/q)>0. Counterexample with vary, v=1, σ_d=1, m=3 gives VoI at r=1,2,3 respectively 0.0000016775, 0.0000235513, 0.0000583861, so the increments increase from 0.0000218738 to 0.0000348348. This posterior is realizable with τ²=2, σ_d²=1 and suitable H. Diminishing returns holds at m=0 because VoI=qφ(0) and q is concave, but is not global. Values concern the Bayes response, not the frozen D̄>0 harness response (T2:14,49).

## Proposition 3 — **Correct with added assumptions**

Claim: fixed-policy conditional VoI tends to zero as σ_t→0, while vary-policy VoI has a positive limit for each fixed H when τ,σ_s>0 (T2:51–57). Let A=τ²+σ_s², u=σ_t². For fixed policy Cov(Δ,D̄|H)=τ²u/(A+u) and Var(D̄|H)=u(1+1/r−u/(A+u)); hence q²=O(u) when A>0. If A=0, Δ is constant and VoI=0. For vary policy, as u→0, v→τ²σ_s²/A>0 and q²→v²/(v+σ_s²/r)>0, with m finite at fixed H. Consequently qψ(|m|/q) tends to a strictly positive value. The bound is pointwise in H, not uniform over all H: VoI tends to zero as |H|→∞. Fix finite μ, τ, σ_s and r; use the common independence assumptions. T2:55's appeal to covariance continuity at a singular limit is insufficient by itself; the explicit conditional calculation handles it.

## Proposition 4 — **Correct with added assumptions**

Claim: for candidate-incumbent normal errors the conditional keep probability is Φ((δ−(1−ϱ)e)/(σ√(1−ϱ²))), and with independent fresh null draws the record diverges and fixed δ acceptance vanishes (T2:65–77). For centered equal-variance bivariate normal errors with σ>0, −1<ϱ<1 and fixed δ, ε_c|e~N(ϱe,σ²(1−ϱ²)); the displayed expression follows and is strictly decreasing in e. At ϱ=1 the covariance is singular and acceptance is deterministic 1{δ>0}; ϱ=−1 likewise requires separate handling. The recorded σ and correlation must refer to a conditional kernel given the actual history: a selected incumbent error need not retain the unconditional bivariate normal law.

For (b), assume independent fresh N(0,σ²) candidate errors at **every** null trial, independent of the past, and a finite initial record. The record is the maximum of that initial value and n fresh draws (exactly n i.i.d. draws only under a specified initial indexing); it tends to infinity a.s., and its expected maximum is asymptotic to σ√(2 log n). The probability in T2:77 must be P(keep at n | past) or P(keep | e_n), a random quantity; the unconditional probability is deterministic and also tends to zero by dominated convergence. The claim applies to a hypothetical fixed-δ proposal after the null stretch, not an arbitrary campaign with improvements or adaptive correlated noise.

## Proposition 5 — **Gap** (index consequence versus hard-budget optimum)

Claim: at E2 verify when VoI_r(H)>2rg; VoI≤qφ(0)≤√vφ(0), so the stated index never verifies when 2rg≥√vφ(0) (T2:90–98). ψ(z)≤φ(0), q≤√v establish the bound, and if the value of a displaced run is an externally fixed additive price g, the comparison is the correct one-step Bayes-response decision. Equality does not trigger a strict-inequality rule. One fresh proposal must cost exactly one run, have the stationary law, and be available without another binding resource for g=V(t*) to be a per-run opportunity value.

The stronger sentence “a fixed budget is better spent on fresh proposals” (T2:98) does not follow as a hard-budget global optimum: integer remaining budgets, future opportunity sets, call caps, and the frozen verify response change the comparison. Finite-budget opportunity cost need not equal constant g. T2:96's implementability concerns the binary action only, whereas the VoI formula assumes a Bayes response and the harness uses D̄>0 (T2:14,49); it is an index proxy, not established actual response value or optimality. Numerical table inputs are not fully specified at T2:100–103, so its numerical values are not independently checked here.

## Proposition 6 — **Correct with added assumptions**

Claim: conditional on pre-execution history, use a per-proposal Gaussian posterior threshold t*=−μσ²/τ², assign arbitrary keep at H=0, and attain (1−π)V(t*) (T4:19–40). On Iᶜ with τ>0 and σ≥0, H has a continuous law, so H=0 identifies the no-op a.s.; for H≠0 the posterior mean is μ+τ²(H−μ)/(τ²+σ²), and maximizing its product with k gives the threshold. No-op value is zero, yielding the mixture value. At σ=0, the zero threshold is optimal regardless of μ; thus the iff reading at T4:42 requires σ>0 and π<1. At τ=0 the finite threshold formula fails and the known-mean cases of Proposition 1 apply; at π=1 the normal component and t* need not be defined. Conditioning on a=1 is meaningful only where it has positive conditional probability. F_n contains the current effective edit and incumbent (T4:19–21), so if no-op status is actually measurable from them then π is 0 or 1 a.s.; a nontrivial π requires an explicit information limitation/coarser F_n. Restrict to Dᶜ as T4:22 states.

## Proposition 7 — **Correct with added assumptions**

Claim: for a finite history-measurable threshold, the loss relative to t* has the oriented integral and quadratic density bounds at T4:46–54. From V′(u)=−f(u)(τ²/s²)(u−t*), integration from t̃ to t* gives the nonnegative oriented integral, including when t̃>t*. Bounding the density over the closed interval with endpoints min(t̃,t*) and max(t̃,t*) yields both inequalities; continuity gives the displayed second-order local expansion. Require τ>0, π<1, finite thresholds, s>0, and the Gaussian assumptions of Proposition 6; the notation [t̃,t*] must mean the unordered-endpoint interval. On no-ops actual threshold decisions can differ but contribute zero value. T4:60's assertion that an upper bound from a pilot predictor bounds the improvement of any better predictor additionally requires validated error/control of the predicted true parameters; the pure algebra does not establish that empirical claim.

## Proposition 8 — **Gap**

Claim: with DI, execute iff V*≥g and the expected summed gain over always execute is EΣ(g−V*)⁺ (T4:64–70). If continuation values at E1 are truly invariant to execution/keep and the baseline and screening policies induce the same relevant exogenous history distribution, immediate maximization gives max(V*−g,0), and subtracting the baseline produces the stated gain along that common distribution. This is a Lagrangian, not hard-budget, result (T4:26–28). Positive-variance and duplicate restrictions from Proposition 6 remain necessary; π=1 permits the separately defined V*=0.

The stated DI in T4:99 conditions on H even when a=0, although H is only observed after executing (T4:25). It does not establish that acquiring H cannot improve a later decision. A signal H can predict future increments while its conditional future law given H is invariant to the current action; execute then reveals H for future decisions whereas skip does not. The branch-copy argument in T4:103 assumes the same available information in both branches, which is unproved. Require a policy-uniform, intervention-defined continuation invariance at the actual E1 information set, including the information revealed by execution, and common support/feasible branch definitions. The expectation in the gain formula must specify the history distribution; pointwise differences alone do not justify summing under different policy-induced histories.

## Proposition 9 — **Correct with added assumptions**

Claim: conditional on a keep, e′ has the stated truncated normal mean and exceeds max(e−Δ,ϱe); this gives the null and e=0 cases (T4:81–93). With σ_c>0, ξ independent of (F,e,Δ), write c=e−Δ and m=ϱe; E[ε|ε>c]=m+σ_cφ((c−m)/σ_c)/Φ(−(c−m)/σ_c). A nondegenerate normal conditional on ε>c has mean greater than both c and m; the two corollaries follow. They hold pointwise almost surely in the assumed conditional model, including adaptive proposal laws only insofar as they preserve the stated independent-noise kernel. At σ=0 or ϱ=1 the conditioning event may be null and the strict inequalities/formula need not hold. T4:93 incorrectly says Δ is F-measurable: A1 generally makes it random given F; conditioning on Δ is sufficient. This describes selected conditional bias, not a guarantee that all adaptive proposers encounter keeps or that unconditional bias rises, and excludes no-ops as defined at T4:83.

## Proposition 10 — **Gap** in (a); **correct** existence example in (b)

Claim (a): DI as defined at T4:99 makes myopic policies globally optimal. The backward induction at T4:103 is valid under stronger action-independent continuation kernels and the same future information available in all compared branches. The written observational conditional law is not that assumption: it conditions on a possibly unobserved H after skip, has support/positivity issues for deterministic policies, and does not ensure the counterpart continuation can access H. Moreover distributions of future observables across an entire trajectory may themselves depend on later policy choices, so a policy-uniform interventional definition is needed. The E1 information-value issue in Proposition 8 is a substantive gap, not merely notation. This claim does not establish optimality under hard run budgets.

Claim (b): in the two-trial noiseless example T4:105, the discarded branch earns τφ(0), and the kept branch earns −α+βΦ(β/τ)+τφ(β/τ); for τ=1, β=2 their difference is approximately 1.60955−α and grows without bound as β→∞. This proves an existence result for a decision-dependent proposal law, with a degenerate known first increment and σ=0 limiting case, not the nondegenerate threshold formula of Proposition 6. T4:108–110's broader claims about detecting all DI violations, necessity of RL, or a small observed violation warranting a ladder decision do not follow from this example or the theorem.
