# Track X D3 isolation redesign — design only, 31 October 2026

**Status: PROPOSAL / BLOCK.** No new fixture, experiment, model, GPU or privileged operation is authorized or executed by this document. Existing `/tmp/d3_namespace_fixture_20261031_d61db5e9b02188f7` remains STOP: do not restart, alter, clean, signal or reuse it. Stage A, P.3a and B.2 remain STOP. The author has authorized design and independent review only; any fixture requires a separately reviewed exact implementation and author approval before execution.

## 1. Evidence and problem

The v18 synthetic fixture independently recorded network-only `setns` denied with errno 1, but opening user/network namespace descriptors and entering user then network both succeeded for an external same-UID probe. The supervisor exited with STOP; subsequent read-only receipt review found initially observed fixture processes no longer present, cause unproven. `/proc/self/mountinfo` after the STOP matched the saved pre-run SHA-256 `45b28845b81e34ab9342db919fbfd71a71335ba3dddbbe60acae306e5adcb932`. Prior 1→2→1 synthetic process counts and temporary synthetic-lock continuity are partial observations only. See `docs/r1/reports/EXAM_D3_NAMESPACE_FIXTURE_STOP_20261031.md` at coordinator commit `72cb318` and `docs/r1/reviews/EXAM_D3_NAMESPACE_FIXTURE_V18_PREUSE_REVIEW_20261031.md`.

TASL-2 current-user snapshots found `/proc/<pid>/exe` inaccessible for hundreds of host PIDs, including non-kernel tasks; passwordless sudo was unavailable. `unshare --user --map-root-user --net true` passed, but that did not establish isolation. Under [Linux user_namespaces(7)](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man7/user_namespaces.7.html), the parent-namespace eUID that created a child user namespace is its owner and has capabilities in it; [setns(2)](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/setns.2.html) specifies the relevant capability checks. This explains why network-only denial cannot establish denial of user→network entry. Permissions on files, a random loopback port, merely dropping capabilities inside the child, or `SO_PEERCRED` checking the same shared UID do not by themselves exclude other processes of that UID.

The author allows the one-resident-server condition to be scoped to the isolated user+network namespace, with host-wide count explicitly **NOT CHECKABLE**, but did not waive namespace isolation, GPU/server compatibility, the real GPU lease, independent review or STOP preservation (`R1-EXAM-SERVER-NAMESPACE-SCOPE`, `docs/r1/PLAN.md`).

## 2. Threat model and proof boundaries

- An untrusted process under the ordinary portal account may attempt same-UID access to namespace handles, filesystem paths, process memory/FDs, same-UID socket credentials, ports, inherited or leaked FDs, worker/server processes and their output. It must be prevented from making inference requests to or changing the serving path during the cold-true and false measurements. Other host users' workloads and files are out of scope for control, and must not be touched.
- Trust: kernel, the administrator-controlled launcher/account boundary, service UID access policy, pinned helper/runtime, and the coordinator/worker as reviewed. Administrator or kernel compromise is not excluded. A privileged host administrator remains able to enter namespaces or alter execution; we make no security claim against it. If administrator activity cannot be excluded during the measured interval, disclose that assumption and treat proof as limited, not absolute.
- Required measurement claim is **one dedicated serving process and no unrecorded generation request within the controlled namespace boundary from freshly verified startup through recorded model shutdown**, with scoped access restrictions checked throughout. Do not claim host-global single-server count; process sampling alone cannot prove continuous absence between samples. Startup side effects and all accepted request routes must be included: a supposedly harmless model/health endpoint is not deemed non-generating without reviewed, pinned binary evidence.
- Exact bytes and bodies: false for every normal Track X request; only the author-prescribed descriptive true block is an explicit exception and must be first on its own fresh server, for the same 50 frozen inputs. Qualification services, fit, template, grammar, observer and errors must have a reviewed request map; Stage A only uses its independently verified paper code. B1/B2 additionally wait for Track S owner’s independently reviewed harness pin and observer error contract. No new theory or evidence claim follows from fixture success.

## 3. Candidate route A: administrator-provisioned separate service identity

**Preferred for a concrete trust boundary, conditional on administrator support; not established available.** A privileged administrator creates a dedicated, project-scoped host service UID, not the shared `dowling` UID, and a reviewed bounded launch mechanism. The service identity (or privileged manager acting for it) creates the target user+network namespace, PID/mount namespaces if required, and the server/worker. The initial-parent namespace owner identity of the target user namespace is verified (for example NS_GET_OWNER_UID where supported); it must not be the shared account. Service UID and namespace owner must not match an ordinary same-UID adversary. Do not assume a service UID's existence or permissions. Current `sudo -n` is unavailable; obtaining privileges, admin changes, setuid binaries, group/device policy or a persistent service is outside current authorization.

Control and data traverse explicitly reviewed IPC. **The current coordinator runs as the ordinary shared `dowling` UID and is not a trusted identity for authentication.** Another process under that UID may impersonate it, borrow a descriptor or credential, or interfere with its process subject to host ptrace policy. Merely restricting the IPC schema, testing `SO_PEERCRED` for the shared UID, using a 0700 directory, or trusting a secret in the shared-UID coordinator does not establish authorized origin. For route A the administrator must provision a distinct, protected coordinator/control identity or perform each launch through an administrator-controlled mechanism outside the shared UID, with no reusable authorization capability exposed to `dowling`. Its credential and service UID, IPC endpoint ownership/mode, process ancestry and ptrace/FD-access restrictions must be reviewed and verified. The protected principal may launch only an explicitly approved immutable job specification with a one-attempt identifier and anti-replay binding; it cannot request an arbitrary shell or namespace entry. How the human author authorizes that protected principal and how secrets and approval metadata are delivered without traversing the untrusted shared UID is an **open design gate**; do not treat author chat approval alone as authenticated access to a host privileged service. A peer with the same UID as any protected coordinator process is likewise inside that process identity boundary unless a separate kernel-enforced policy is independently shown. A trusted service UID used simultaneously by untrusted processes would also invalidate this design. Namespace handles, pidfds, privileged credentials, raw GPU device FDs, listening sockets and service account credentials are not leaked to the ordinary account. The launch policy should reject any parent/worker/server identity mismatch, unexpected descriptor, unexpected ancestry, additional listening/generation process or unauthorized route. Avoid claiming arbitrary protection just from file mode 0700: it must be combined with actual separate host identity and tested proc/ptrace/namespace-handle permissions. Strong authentication and per-request identity/order binding must remain inside the trusted boundary. If a gateway is used it must prove that the model server cannot be reached directly by other namespace members, not merely log its own requests.

**Protected inputs and check-to-use binding.** The already-published prompt bundle on portal is owned by shared `dowling` and is a receipt copy, not tamper-resistant executable input. After separate administrator and author approval, a trusted administrator-side principal must copy the exact approved prompt, five ledgers, code, model and configuration into a service-UID-protected area that shared-UID peers cannot write or rename. Verify owner, mode, parent-directory ownership, filesystem/mount trust, expected SHA-256 and source provenance on the protected bytes, including all referenced runtime/dependency artifacts. Reject symlinks, hard-link aliasing or traversals unless separately proven safe; use an appropriate reviewed `openat2`/directory-FD resolution policy or equivalent. Atomic publication after validation or immutable/sealed FD-backed content can bind approval to use, but read-only FDs or a read-only mount of files still writable by another process do **not** by themselves prevent changes to underlying bytes. The final loader, interpreter imports, model loader and every reopened path must consume those same protected bytes or an independently proved immutable equivalent. Recheck the post-open identity/hash and exact consuming FD/object immediately before use, and reject any race, replacement, unaccounted import, executable mismatch, mutable external mount or inability to bind bytes; a hash followed by an ordinary path reopen is insufficient. Record the protected object identities and digests and independently review the chain from author-approved bytes to actual consuming process. Stage A remains BLOCK until tested end-to-end; do not claim this property from the prompt bundle's existing hash or mode 0600.

**Process-observation scope.** Inaccessible or hidden host-global processes remain **NOT CHECKABLE** and are disclosed as outside the author-approved namespace scope; no host-global single-server claim may be made. Within the controlled namespace, however, every in-scope process/server and relevant namespace must be visible under the reviewed design; an inaccessible, hidden, ambiguous, newly appearing, or unaccounted process/connection or loss of monitoring coverage is STOP, not a skipped PID or a namespace PASS. Discrete samples are not continuity proof.

Risk needing design: a `setns` attacker with ordinary UID must fail at opening or joining protected user/net namespace; the test distinguishes each syscall and its exact errno. Admin-only bypass, alternate proc/pidfd paths, `/proc/PID/ns/*`, open namespace FDs, inherited FDs, service worker poisoning, process injection and filesystem permission paths must be enumerated. A denial in one path is not global proof. Protected syscall/event mechanisms and loss monitoring may need privileged policy; do not silently substitute snapshots for continuous auditing.

The shared, existing `~/r1_locks/gpu0.lock` is not to be renamed, replaced or deleted. A reviewed **administrator-controlled**, trusted lock-acquiring process (not the untrusted shared-UID coordinator) and service handoff must use **the same filesystem inode and open-file description** that `flock` uses, verify the original inode/device, and prove the actual locked file description reaches each intended keeper. Record a reviewed `fork`/`dup`/`exec` inheritance chain or authenticated `SCM_RIGHTS` transfer and, where authorized and supported, test cross-process open-file-description equality with `kcmp(KCMP_FILE)` while both processes are alive. `kcmp` may fail due to ptrace/LSM/kernel permissions; if it is unavailable or denied, an independently reviewed equivalent proof is necessary, otherwise this condition is **NOT CHECKABLE**, not PASS. Device/inode, PID/start tick, `/proc/.../fd` symlink, and an independent contender failure cannot alone establish open-file-description identity: separate opens of the same inode can have different descriptions. [flock(2)](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/flock.2.html) and [kcmp(2)](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/kcmp.2.html) describe these distinctions. Prove a separate independent cooperating contender cannot acquire the lock, and retain the lease over the entire service/worker/STOP lifetime. A detached, separately supervised keeper could hold an inherited duplicate FD; its liveness, PID/start time and `/proc/.../fd` inode must be recorded. It must survive coordinator failure without signaling the service; if keeper death, uncertain ownership, unknown lease inode, a second service or observation loss occurs, STOP. Whether permissions and actual GPU lease semantics permit this is **NOT CHECKABLE** until separately tested. A marker file and one successful synthetic `flock` experiment do not prove the real GPU lock or non-cooperating GPU users are excluded. The availability and identity of real GPU devices must also be checked without disturbing others, before an independently authorized real job.

## 4. Alternative routes and decision boundaries

**B. Administrator-managed network/service sandbox without a separate namespace owner.** E.g. a unit in a root-controlled namespace, firewall/LSM policy or a dedicated container runtime may prevent ingress. Feasibility and administrator authorization are unknown; it is acceptable only if independently reviewed to establish a *kernel-enforced* same-UID adversary boundary and all required socket/FD paths, not just a process filter or a monitoring rule. No firewall, LSM, daemon, or host configuration may be changed under the design-only authorization. It may have different production-availability and operational costs from A.

**C. Cooperative-account / bounded observation only.** Reuse the current shared UID and assume other same-UID processes cooperate; keep complete client journals and enumerate the accessible namespace. This does **not** prove exclusion and cannot pass the current cold/single-service gate. It would require an explicit author protocol amendment defining the assumption, the weaker claim, consequences and independent review; do not adopt or execute it merely because A/B are difficult. An author waiver cannot create missing runtime evidence; disclose the scope and limit scientifically.

Do not relabel the old failed fixture, promote it to a pass, infer no unauthorized calls from absence in llama-server/Ollama logs, or change Stage A data/selection to mask the issue. There is no need to choose A/B/C before independent design review and an author decision about administrator access.

## 5. Proposed validation programme — not authorized to run

Each test uses separately reviewed, byte-pinned scripts and a prereviewed input/command/expected-output manifest, a unique new private output directory and exclusively created **synthetic** resources. An independent reviewer checks complete source, command argv, exact paths, all referenced executable/library hashes, mount propagation and cleanup/STOP design before any run. Capture full original stdout/stderr/errno and loss counters; never treat an inaccessible path as absence. No tests reuse the stopped fixture. Normal fixture-owned processes may exit by their protocol; never kill, signal, restart or modify an existing job, and any STOP preserves its artifacts and remaining live processes. Before any mount, prove a new private mount namespace, set and confirm private propagation and preserve host mountinfo; a setup that cannot guarantee this is rejected without mounting.

1. **Nonprivileged topology preflight.** With no privileged or real GPU actions, independently establish expected namespace ownership, UID mappings, host process visibility limits, procfs visibility, PID/start-time identity and event source/loss behavior. Distinguish PID exit races, EACCES and kernel threads. Hidden, inaccessible or unclassifiable host-global processes are **NOT CHECKABLE** and disclosed outside the author-approved namespace scope; they are not by themselves a namespace STOP or evidence of absence. Within the controlled namespace, an unaccounted, hidden, inaccessible or unclassifiable process, or loss of observation, is STOP. Pin OS/kernel/cgroup/LSM policy and every evidence helper; snapshots alone yield only snapshot claims. The initial same-UID attack that succeeded in the old fixture must remain a regression negative control.
2. **Provisioned identity and namespace adversarial fixture.** Only after author and administrator separately approve the exact provisioned UID/launcher and isolated test, use new synthetic resources. First validate a protected coordinator principal independent of the shared UID and the author-to-principal authorization channel. Include negative controls in which another shared-UID process attempts to impersonate the coordinator, use or steal its IPC credentials or inherited FDs, use ptrace/process memory/FD access, replay an old authorization, or mutate/replace/symlink the approved script, manifest, prompts or protected copies between verification and consumption; any successful impersonation, mutable-input attack, missing identity binding or undecidable result is STOP. Trusted positive controls must prove the harness detects deliberately substituted bytes and rejects them; test paths and actual bytes must be independently receipted. Verify namespace identity/owner by kernel interface and privileges, syscall return/errno at each `open`, `setns(user)`, `setns(net)`, and target access stage. Attempt original same-UID user→net path, network-only path, permitted fd paths and process/FD injection where applicable; distinguish denied syscall from missing resources or failure after successful entry. Every attempted access that should be denied must demonstrate the denial for the intended reason. Independently controlled positive controls must prove the probe can enter a deliberately permitted fixture-owned namespace; and a two-server negative control must be detected. No connection to an unowned port: pre-bind and validate fixture-owned listeners; collision is STOP, never retry.
3. **Cold/order/transport CPU fixture.** On fixture-only fake servers and 50 clearly synthetic prompts, validate the approved ordering (fresh service, true-first, each requested repeat and free/schema identity; tokenization/prefill and template order) and exact raw-byte preservation, with explicit false on relevant requests. Journal must bind request/response body bytes and SHA, route, server identity, monotonic time, prompt/seed/mode/repeat and error, and report on-disk writes safely. Compare exact request content except intentionally varied fields. Test deliberate additional request, unauthorized sender, extra server, stale service, wrong seed/schema/cache setting, reordered or missing request, same-owner namespace entry and injected I/O errors including short write, ENOSPC and incomplete read. Each negative must cause STOP without disappearing raw evidence; if logging itself fails, propagate STOP without requiring a second successful write.
4. **Synthetic lock/lifecycle fixture.** Without touching the real GPU lock, explicitly test the administrator-controlled lock acquisition, recorded `fork`/`dup`/`exec` or authenticated `SCM_RIGHTS` transfer and cross-process `kcmp(KCMP_FILE)` or separately reviewed equivalent, plus independent contender after supervisor natural exit, holder loss, resource-margin failure, lock file inode swap and recorded adversarial STOP. Include a negative control with independently opened FDs to the same inode and require the proof to reject them; simulate `EPERM`/unavailable `kcmp` and require NOT CHECKABLE/STOP. PID, inode and contender evidence alone cannot satisfy description equality. Ensure namespace PID 1 does not exit and kill surviving fixture processes on error; no `subprocess.run(timeout=...)` auto-kill, no `Popen` destructor cleanup that signals children, no PID reuse confusion, and no stale-lock recovery or resumed RUNNING/FAILED batch. Synthetic success is not actual GPU-lock evidence.
5. **Post-run independent receiving.** Before considering any further phase, a fresh reviewer recomputes hashes, IDs, sequence, sample times, journal completeness across files, namespace/UID ownership, request coverage, probe controls and lock ownership, examines logs and explicitly enumerates gaps. Unexpected test exit, denied setup, inability to demonstrate isolation, post-run host mountinfo difference, any unknown or inaccessible process within the controlled namespace, loss of in-scope observation, any failed negative or mismatch → STOP and preserve new fixture; no automatic retry. Host-global hidden or inaccessible processes are **NOT CHECKABLE** and disclosed outside the author-approved namespace scope; they are not by themselves a namespace STOP or evidence of absence. `NOT CHECKABLE` is not PASS.
6. **Later production-compatibility gate, only under a separate explicit author decision.** After accepted CPU fixture, approve a new isolated, bounded method for verifying GPU device/NVML visibility and exact pinned server startup/response behavior, real lock FD continuity, server residency and network access in the proposed identity boundary. Obtain fresh GPU census and operator-coordination evidence first; no other user's job may be preempted. No real Stage A run follows automatically from synthetic tests. A production feasibility STOP is preserved as its own separate attempt.
