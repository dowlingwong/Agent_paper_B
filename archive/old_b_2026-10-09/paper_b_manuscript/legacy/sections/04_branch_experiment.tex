\section{Branch experiment and checkpoint instrumentation}
\label{sec:branch}

The root trajectory is the independent experimental unit.  A root is assigned
to $J=0$ (sham) or $J=1$ (injected fault) before eligible checkpoint selection.
Exactly one checkpoint is chosen by randomized eligible checkpoint selection
within the registered window of each root.  This prevents multiple checkpoints
with shared ancestry from being counted as independent units in the primary
analysis.  At the selected checkpoint, the version-locked availability registry
is evaluated from pre-treatment evidence and its structured decisions are
recorded, a restorable snapshot is frozen, and every feasible registered regime
is executed.  Downstream
repetitions within checkpoint--regime cells measure stochastic variation but
do not increase the number of roots, and roots sharing a task template are not
independent: the template is the independent unit.

Branch order and workload seeds are randomized by a registered scheduler.
Where appropriate, the same workload seed couples regimes within a repetition
to reduce variance while leaving each regime's marginal estimand unchanged.
The pinned local runtime records sampler settings and model seeds, but no
deterministic claim is made unless the production capability audit demonstrates
it.  Any uncoupled model randomness is recorded as such.

Instrumentation records experiment, template, root, checkpoint, branch,
regime, repetition, ancestry, availability status and reason codes, availability
evidence hash, registry and evaluator versions, fault assignment, workload seed,
model-seed claim, continuation version, verifier result, terminal category,
failure class, outcome, artifact hashes, raw resource use, and timestamps.
A failed restore, incomplete manifest, or cross-branch write is a checkpoint
instrumentation failure and invalidates the checkpoint block.  A regime
declared unavailable before treatment is not assigned.  A failure caused by an
assigned regime remains an outcome.  Missingness after regime initiation is
post-treatment missingness and cannot be recoded as pre-treatment
unavailability.

Inference must preserve the design: branch repetitions are paired within a
checkpoint, roots are the primary clusters, and task-template clustering is
addressed by the registered analysis or by defining templates as fixed strata.

\subsection{Failures that execute cleanly but change the estimand}
\label{app:failure-taxonomy}

Every row of \cref{tab:failure-taxonomy} describes a branch contrast that runs
without error and still estimates something other than the declared comparison.
The controls test \emph{specified} defects; they do not establish universal
detection.  The declared allowance $p$ is a hypothesis carried with its
provenance --- a validated allowance, a structural coupling, or a sensitivity
value --- and the fourth column prices what remains identified if the audit
misses the defect.  Excluding a checkpoint by the registered pre-treatment availability
rule is legitimate even when the analyst applies that already-recorded rule later.
